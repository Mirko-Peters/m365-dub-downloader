Reframing the executive metric, decision velocity, so how do we actually measure the true cost of all this digital debt? We have to stop looking at time-saved as our primary indicator of success because that is a vanity metric. It is misleading, it is easy to game, and in the age of a gentick AI, it is often a total lie. If your team saves 13 minutes on a first draft, but that draft adds two hours to the executive review cycle because it is full of work slop, you haven't gained anything at all. In reality, you have actually lost over an hour of high value human attention. The math of simple efficiency just doesn't work when the output is unreliable. This is why I want you to pivot your entire leadership focus toward a new metric called decision velocity. This is the total time it takes to go from a business question to a trusted, logged, and actionable decision. It doesn't matter how fast the AI can type. What matters is how fast your leadership can act on what the AI produced. If your content volume is going up, but your decision speed is going down, your organization is officially in the red. To track this properly, you need to monitor three specific signals across your Microsoft 365 environment. The first signal is decision cycle time, which is the literal lag between the moment and AI output is generated, and the moment a human owner accepts that output as final. If this gap is widening, it means your people are spending more time auditing the machine than they are using it, and that is a red alert for process rot. The second signal is decision reversals. How often are you revisiting work that was supposed to be finished because the AI input was fundamentally flawed? In a high-dead organization, you will see teams constantly circling back to previous topics because the automated summary they relied on missed a critical legal or technical constraint. Every single reversal acts as a direct penalty on your bottom line. The third signal is what I call the confidence lag, and you can measure this by looking at your shadow spreadsheet index. Ask your department heads a simple question about how many of their people are still keeping manual offline records because they don't trust the live data in the system.

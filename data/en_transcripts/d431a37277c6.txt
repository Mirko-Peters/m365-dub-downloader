What an AI agent actually is. Start with the thing you already know because it makes the rest of this easier. A chatbot waits for your question, gives you an answer, and then stops right there. An agent takes your request and keeps working so it can look something up. Change a record, and then tell you what it did. Picture a helper who handles your travel booking for you. You say you need a trip next month and the helper checks your calendar, finds a flight, books it, and sends you the details. A chatbot would only describe how to book that flight while an agent goes ahead and books it. Plane automation is different again, and it's worth keeping the two apart. Automation follows a fixed set of steps every single time, so it breaks the moment something unexpected happens. An agent decides which step to take next based on whatever it finds along the way, which is why it holds up better when the real world gets messy. If that sounds like a small difference, think about how much of your day disappears into chasing things down. Someone asks for an update and you open three systems to answer. An agent can do that opening and checking for you, and then hand you the answer with the work already done. Every agent needs three simple parts. The first part is a model, which is the AI that reads your request and decides what to do next. The second part is instructions, which act like a job description and tell the agent what it is for. The third part is tools, which are the ways the agent can act, such as searching the web or updating a record. Put those three parts together and you get something that can work through a task in several steps instead of stopping at one reply. That's the whole idea behind every agent you'll meet today, no matter how big the name sounds. The model, it turns out, is the easy part.

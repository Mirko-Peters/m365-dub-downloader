So vector search, this is a completely different way of doing things. Instead of matching exact words, it matches meaning. That sounds abstract. So let's break it down. The simplest definition you need to know is this. A vector is just a list of numbers that represents something a computer can work with. That's it. A list of numbers. An embedding is a special type of vector that captures the meaning of text. It takes a sentence, a paragraph, even a whole document, and turns it into a long list of numbers. The computer doesn't understand the words, but it understands the numbers. Here's an analogy that helps. Think of vectors like coordinates on a map. On a map, similar places are close together. In vector space, similar concepts end up close together. The vector for king and the vector for queen are near each other because they represent related ideas. The vector for king and the vector for apple are far apart. The computer doesn't know what king means in a human sense, but it can measure the distance between its coordinates and the coordinates of every other word or document. It's like having a GPS for ideas. The system knows how far apart concepts are just by looking at the numbers. When you search using vectors, the system converts your query into an embedding. Then it finds the documents whose embeddings are closest to yours. It finds things that are semantically similar that share meaning, not just vocabulary. Azure AI search has three core building blocks, the data source, the indexer and the search index. The data source is where your information lives. It could be blob storage and Azure SQL database, Cosmos DB or even SharePoint. It's the raw material that needs to become searchable. Think of it as the pile of documents waiting to be organized. The indexer is the pipeline that reads data from that source, extracts the content, applies any AI enrichment you've configured and then builds the search index. Here's a simple way to picture it. It's the structured optimized system that lets you find exactly what you need in milliseconds. When you type a query, the system doesn't go back to the original documents and scan through them. Instead, it looks up the index, which already knows exactly where everything is located. This pre-organization is called an inverted index. Instead of storing documents with a list of words they contain, you store words with a list of documents that contain them. That means when you search for a term, you jump straight to the right place. No flipping through every page. The result is a massive speed improvement.

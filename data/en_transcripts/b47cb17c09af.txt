Key vault has two different ways to control access to what's inside, and the one you choose matters a lot. The first model is legacy access policies. This is the old way. Key vault secrets user. This lets someone actually read secret values. And key vault secrets officer. This lets someone create, update, and delete secrets. Notice the pattern. Each role is specific to the data inside the vault, not the vault itself. Now contrast that with the broad roles, owner, contributor, reader. These apply to the vault as a resource, not to the data inside it. An owner can delete the entire vault. A contributor can modify the vault settings. You need access to the vault itself. That's the first layer. And you need access to the data inside the vault. That's the second layer. Both must be granted separately.

Before co-pilot, before we talk about co-pilot, we need to look at the building it works in. Dynamics 365 is Microsoft's connected platform for running parts of a business. It stores the day-to-day facts that teams need to do their jobs. A sales team tracks leads, contacts, accounts, meetings, and possible deals. A customer service team tracks cases, messages, customer problems, and the steps taken to fix them. Finance tracks invoices, payments, budgets, and the movement of money. Operations and supply chain teams track stock, purchase orders, suppliers, warehouses, and deliveries. All of that information answers a simple question. What is happening in the business right now? Many people hear Dynamics 365 and think only about customer relationships. That part is called CRM, which means customer relationship management. CRM is where a company keeps track of people and conversations. A salesperson might open a customer account and see the people they know, previous meetings, open opportunities, emails, and notes from colleagues. Customer service uses the same sort of customer history in a different way. Instead of asking, "Oh, will this deal close?" ERP is the part that tracks the work behind the customer promise. If someone orders a product, ERP can track whether the product is in stock, whether it needs to be bought, what it costs, when it ships, and when the company should send an invoice. So CRM looks toward the customer relationship. ERP looks at the business work required to deliver on that relationship. Think of Dynamics 365 like an office building. Sales works in one room, looking after leads and deals. Customer service works in another room, looking after cases. Finance has its own room for invoices and payments. Operations has rooms for stock, orders, and suppliers. Inside every room sit filing cabinets full of business records. Those records aren't just documents. A customer record can link to an order. An order can link to stock. A service case can link to the customer who bought the product. When the data connects, people don't need to rebuild the story from scratch every time. T-T, what Dynamics 365 Copilot actually does? So what does Copilot actually do when you type a question or open a record? In plain English, Dynamics 365 Copilot is a set of AI tools inside Dynamics 365 apps that helps you understand business data, prepare drafts, and move work forward. It doesn't replace the app. It helps you use the app without spending so much time clicking through pages, reading every note, or starting every message from a blank screen. Most of its day-to-day help falls into three areas. First, it can summarize. A Dynamics record can collect a lot of history. There might be notes from several people, emails, meetings, updates, dates, and changes to fields. Copilot can turn that history into a short explanation of what matters now. Maybe you open an opportunity and want to know what changed since you last looked at it. Instead of reading every activity one by one, you can ask Copilot for a summary of recent changes. It can pull together the current stage, recent activity, and details already stored in the record. The information isn't new. Copilot is helping you see it in a usable form. Second, it can answer questions about the data you can access. You don't always know where the answer lives. Maybe it sits in a customer record. Maybe it is in a case, an order, a workflow, or an approved document linked to the work. Rather than learning every filter and every report menu, you can ask a question in normal words. You might ask, oh, what are the latest changes on this opportunity? Oh, or. Ozemorize this customer case and suggest the next steps. Oh, the question sounds simple because it is simple. Copilot translates that request into a search across the approved business information it can use, then returns an answer in plain language. That doesn't mean every question has the same answer everywhere. A question inside a sales app focuses on sales records. A question inside a finance app works with finance and operations data. The answer depends on the app, the records, and your access. Third, Copilot can help create a first draft or propose an action. This is where many people first notice it. You may need to reply to a customer, write a case resolution note, prepare meeting notes, or fill in part of a form. Copilot can use the context around the work to give you a starting point. A starting point matters. Starting from a blank page sounds small, but it adds up across a week. Copilot can draft the first version while you bring the judgment, the customer knowledge, and the final decision. You still read it. You still change it when it needs changing, and you decide whether it should be sent, saved, or ignored. That human check is not optional, especially when a message reaches a customer or a change affects a business record. AI can misunderstand context, miss a detail, or phrase something in a way your company wouldn't use. Treat a copilot response like work prepared by a junior colleague. It may save you time, but your name still goes on the email, the case update, or the approval. Where do you see it? Sometimes copilot appears as a chat panel beside the app you are already using. Microsoft calls this a sidecar, but you can think of it as a conversation area next to your work. You keep the customer, order, or case open, and then ask a question without leaving the page. Other times, copilot appears directly inside a page. You may see a short record summary at the top of a lead, an opportunity, or a customer page. You may see suggested information while filling in a form. The AI help is part of the screen rather than a separate chat. There are also experiences outside Dynamics 365 itself. Microsoft 365 copilot works across tools such as teams, Outlook, Word, and Excel. It can connect to Dynamics 365 in certain situations, bringing business context into the places where people write emails, attend meetings, and work with documents. The distinction is simple. Dynamics 365 copilot works close to the business process and the records inside Dynamics 365, Microsoft 365. Copilot works close to your everyday Microsoft 365 work such as email, meetings, and documents. They can work together, but they aren't the same product wearing different names.

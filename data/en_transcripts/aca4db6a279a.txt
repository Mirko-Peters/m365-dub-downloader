And every time money moves, it needs a proper place in the company books. Duh. The general ledger, the company, O-T-M-Ters main filing cabinet. Every financial transaction needs a permanent home. In Dynamics 365 finance, that home is the general ledger. Think of it as the company O-T-M-AIDS official filing cabinet. Every movement of money lands in the right drawer with a clear label and a trail back to the original record, a supplier payment belongs somewhere. So does a customer payment, office rent, employee travel, tax, stock costs, and bank fees. The general ledger keeps those records together. It uses rules that let finance see the full financial position of the company instead of chasing separate totals. The drawers in this filing cabinet come from the chart of accounts. A chart of accounts is simply a list of categories the company uses for money. You might see accounts for sales income, rent, software costs, travel, wages, tax, cash in the bank, money owed by customers and money owed to suppliers. Each account answers a basic question. What kind of money movement was this? When the company pays rent, finance posts that amount to the rent account. When it sells a product, finance posts the income to a sales account. When a customer still needs to pay an invoice, that amount sits in an account for money the company expects to collect. That structure keeps the books readable. Without it, finance could see that money moved, but not what the money meant. A bank balance tells you how much cash sits in the account today. It can't tell you where the costs rose because of new staff, a large supplier bill, or an unexpected tax payment. The chart of accounts gives each amount a proper label.

Now let's look at the other side of this choice. How multi-repo's work and their hidden tax. A multi-repo keeps your services, libraries, or products in separate repositories. When Team B updates their service, Team A does not automatically pull in that change. The promise is explicit. Team autonomy. Each team controls their own repository and decides exactly when to deploy. You can iterate at your own pace without coordinating with anyone to push a change. You do not have to wait for code reviews from distant teams, and you do not even have to understand how anyone else's system works. You just control your own corner of the world. The architecture is built on contracts instead of shared code. If Team A needs to call a service owned by Team B, they use an API contract. Team B publishes the API and Team A consumes it. Team B can change their implementation as long as the API stays the same, and Team A can upgrade to a new version on their own schedule. They test it against their own code and deploy whenever they feel ready. This is elegant in theory. You have independent teams, clear interfaces, and minimal coordination.

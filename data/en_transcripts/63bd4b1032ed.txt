A simple audit plan for your organization. A useful audit plan starts before anyone reports a problem. First, make sure audit is enabled, then confirm that records are actually arriving. Next, separate the jobs. An audit reader searches records and reviews the results. An audit manager handles the settings, retention policies, and the wider care of the audit service. Those roles don't need to sit with the same person. In fact, separating them often gives you better control. The person who searches a record doesn't always need the power to change how long that record stays in the system. Sensitive cases need another boundary. E-discovery case access should stay separate from general audit access. A person may need to confirm that a file was shared without needing permission to open a legal case or review private documents. Give people the access their work requires and no more.

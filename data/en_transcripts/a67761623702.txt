Consumption versus standard, which model fits you. You understand what logic apps is, how triggers and actions work, and the power of connectors. But there's another decision you need to make that affects how much you pay. Logic apps comes in two flavors, consumption and standard, and they're not the same thing, so choosing the wrong one can cost you. Consumption is the pay per execution model. You build your workflow and only pay when it runs. If it sits idle all day, you pay nothing. If it runs a hundred times, you pay for a hundred executions. The first 4,000 actions each month are free, and after that it's fractions of a penny per action. Connector calls cost a bit more, but still tiny amounts. For most beginners, this is the right place to start. Think of consumption like a taxi. You pay for the ride you take. If you don't go anywhere, you don't pay. If you take a short trip, it costs a little. If you take a long trip, it costs more. The price matches your usage exactly. Standard is different. It runs on dedicated compute, like having your own server, but managed by Microsoft. You pay a fixed monthly cost based on how much compute power you reserve. The smallest standard plan starts around $175 a month and gives you one virtual CPU and 3.5 gigabytes of memory. You can run as many workflows as you want on that plan, and the price doesn't change based on how many times they execute. Standard is like a car payment. You pay the same amount every month, whether you drive it once a day or once a year. If you drive a lot, that fixed cost can be cheaper per trip than taking taxis everywhere. But if you barely drive, you're paying for capacity you don't use. So when do you pick each one? Consumption is great for spiky or low volume workloads. Say a workflow that runs a few times a day when someone uploads a file, that's pennies per month on consumption. So putting it on standard would be a waste. But if you have a workflow that runs thousands of times in our processing orders, handling real-time data, or orchestrating complex integrations, the per execution costs of consumption add up fast. At that point, standards fixed monthly price becomes the cheaper option. There's another difference worth knowing. Standard gives you more control. You get better performance because the compute is dedicated to you, plus virtual network support for strict security requirements. And you can even run standard workflows locally on your own hardware. Consumption runs only in Microsoft's cloud on shared infrastructure, so you don't get the same level of control, but you also don't have to manage anything. For most people starting out, the answer is simple. Start with consumption. It costs virtually nothing to try.

So now that you know how to stay safe, the question is, where should you actually run these experiments? Shifting left versus shifting right. So where do you actually run these experiments? There are two main approaches and most teams use both. The first is shifting left. You run chaos experiments early in your development life cycle. As soon as code gets deployed to a test environment, you hit it with faults and make it part of your CICD pipeline. The pipeline deploys the app runs a chaos experiment to stress it, checks that it survives, and only then promotes the build to the next stage. Think of it as a quality gate, just like unit tests or security scans. If the app can't handle a VM shutdown in staging, it doesn't go to production. The second approach is shifting right, which means running experiments in production. And yes, that sounds terrifying, but hear me out. You don't start there. You only shift right after you've built confidence in non-production environments. You begin with small, limited experiments. Maybe you stress CPU on one instance during a low traffic period. You watch closely, you learn, and then you expand. Maybe you shut down a single VM in production during business hours and validate that your load balancer actually re-route traffic, that your users don't notice a thing, and that your monitoring catches it all. Over time, you build up to more aggressive experiments. There's a third approach that sits in the middle, game days. A game day is a scheduled chaos drill, where you bring together your development team, operations team, security team, everyone who would be involved in a real incident. You run a chaos experiment and practice your incident response. Who gets the alert, who makes the call to escalate? What's the communication chain? How fast can you diagnose the issue? Game days test the technology, but more importantly, they test the people and processes around it. The key idea across all three approaches is that chaos engineering is not a one-time test. You don't run one experiment, declare your system resilient, and move on. Systems change, code changes, configurations change, dependencies change.

Now let's talk about the two flavors of managed identities because this is where people get confused. They're not the same thing and picking the wrong one can cause headaches later. The core difference comes down to one word, life cycle. Who owns the identity's lifespan? Who decides when it's created and when it's destroyed? System assigned is the simpler of the two. You enable it on a resource, a virtual machine, an app service, a single function, and Azure creates an identity that is tied directly to that resource. They share a life when the resource is born, the identity is born. When you delete the resource, the identity is deleted too, one to one relationship. The beauty of System assigned is that there's almost no management overhead. You flip a switch in the portal or add a single line in your bicep template, and it's done. No separate resource to create, no cleanup to worry about. If you have a single web app that needs to read from its own storage account, this is your answer. Enable it, grant the role, move on, but there's a catch because the identity is tied to that specific resource. If you delete the resource, the identity disappears. That's fine if you're done with it. But what if you're migrating workloads? What if you need to rebuild a VM and want to keep the same identity? With System assigned, you'd have to re-enable it and re-ground permissions. Not a disaster, but an extra step. User assigned solves that problem. Here you create the identity as a standalone Azure resource first. It exists independently with its own life cycle. That's incredibly useful when you have a shared development cluster where multiple services all need access to the same key vault. Instead of granting permissions five times to five different identities, you grant it once to a user assigned identity and attach it everywhere. So when do you pick which? Here's a simple framework.

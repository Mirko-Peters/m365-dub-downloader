In 2026, the answer is event-driven architecture. Moving from scripts to platforms is really a shift in how you think about time. In the old model, you think in batches. You do work on a schedule. This is where graph subscriptions become critical. A subscription means that when something changes in Microsoft 365, your system hears about it immediately. A new email arrives. A file gets updated. What happens when a thousand events arrive at once and your system isn't ready? The answer is a queue. Events hit your subscription endpoint. But your logic app doesn't process them yet. It drops them into a service bus queue. Then a separate system drains that queue at whatever pace it can actually handle. Spikes and activity don't crash your system because the queue absorbs the shock. Your system just keeps processing steadily, no matter how many events are piling up. This is where platform thinking gets interesting. You aren't processing things one to one anymore. You're moving to an asynchronous model where the queue provides the resilience. If your processing system goes down, the queue stays up. When the system comes back online, it just picks up where it left off and clears the backlog. The Delta query we talked about earlier fits right into this. It isn't just for reconciliation. It's the safety net for when a subscription misses an event. The subscription handles the real-time flow. The Delta query runs once a day to catch the gaps. Your processing system doesn't care where the data came from.

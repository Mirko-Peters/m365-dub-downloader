The structural reality, why this matters. Everything we've walked through over the last hour comes down to one thing, understanding the model, not the products, not which agent you pick for which task, the model. Because there's a shift happening that doesn't make headlines the way new features do, it's quieter, more structural, and it determines whether your agent investments actually work. The shift is from AI as a feature to AI as infrastructure. Features are things you add to existing products. You add copilot to your office apps. It's useful, but it's still word, word is the system. And copilot is just the feature bolted on. Infrastructure is different. Infrastructure is what systems are built on. It's the foundation. It's what everything else rests on and operates through. Agents are transitioning from the first to the second and that changes what organizations actually need to do. When agents were features, you didn't need to think about layers. You didn't need governance. You didn't need to worry about identity or orchestration. You turned on copilot. It worked and you were done. But the moment agents start operating in your systems, they stop being features. They call your APIs. They access your data. They make decisions that affect your business. They become infrastructure. And infrastructure requires thinking about foundations. That's what the four layers actually represent. The experience layer is where humans interact. That's the interface. That's the front door. Most organizations start here because they see a team's bought or a scout agent and think that's the whole picture. But it's not. It's the symptom, not the system. The agent layer is where domain expertise operates. This is where you define what an agent actually does. Security agent, sales agent, finance agent. Each one specializes because specialization means accuracy. But this layer by itself is just logic. It can't execute anything without what comes next. The runtime layer is where execution actually happens. Your logic needs somewhere to run, somewhere to maintain state, somewhere to call tools and coordinate with other agents, somewhere to scale. That's the runtime. It's the infrastructure the experience layer depends on and the agent layer operates within. The governance layer is what keeps everything safe and visible. Identity, policy, audit, life cycle. These aren't optional add-ons once you're operating at scale. They're foundational. An agent without identity is invisible. An agent without policy is uncontrolled. An agent without audit is unmeasurable. These are the constraints that transform agents from experimental features into systems organizations contrast. Here's what actually matters. Each layer has different concerns. Each layer requires different thinking. Confusing them is where most deployments break. Organizations that succeed understand this. They know that an agent is the experience plus the logic plus the infrastructure plus the governance. All four, not one, not two, all four working together. Identity is the foundation because you can't govern what you can't identify. Entra agent ID makes agents first class principles. That sounds technical. What it actually means is agents become manageable. You know what they are. You can control what they do. You can audit what they did. Orchestration is the multiplier because a single agent is useful. But multiple agents coordinating are transformative. But coordination only works if you have infrastructure designed for it. That's the runtime layer. Foundry isn't just a place to host agents. It's where orchestration becomes possible. Understanding this model isn't optional. It's what separates organizations building agent infrastructure from organizations experimenting with agent features. One scales, the other doesn't.

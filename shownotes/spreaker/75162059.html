Many factories run on a planner who stitches together ERP dates, spreadsheets, whiteboards and phone calls until the plan breaks. This episode helps manufacturing and operations leaders recognize the warning signs that manual planning has hit its limit and shows how to introduce planning software that produces feasible, explainable schedules.

Key takeaways

  • Production planning software becomes necessary when changes, shared resources and dependencies outpace what planners can test manually, not when the company reaches a certain size.
  • ERP owns demand and materials and MES records execution, while a planning layer must test feasible sequences against real capacity, setup, material and qualification constraints.
  • Start with one failing decision, a clear process and trusted product, process and resource data, run the new plan in parallel, and use Fabric, Power BI and Power Platform around the engine, not instead of it.

Worth quoting

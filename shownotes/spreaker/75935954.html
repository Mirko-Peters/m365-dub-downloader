Passwords and one-time codes can still be phished, and Microsoft is now making passkeys the default sign-in for Microsoft 365. This Simply Explained episode is for everyday users and IT admins who want to understand passkeys and FIDO2 without the jargon. You will learn how passkeys work, which type to choose and what to plan for before rolling them out.

  • 08:25 – When your phone or key goes missing
  • 10:05 – Passkeys in Microsoft 365 and Azure
  • 12:25 – The honest catches and a recap

Key takeaways

  • A passkey is a key pair whose private half never leaves your device, so there is no shared secret that a phishing page could steal.
  • Synced passkeys suit most users because they survive a lost device, while administrators and privileged accounts should use device-bound passkeys such as security keys or Microsoft Authenticator.
  • Passkeys are becoming the default sign-in in Microsoft Entra ID, so set up a backup passkey early and plan for limits such as visibility of synced copies.

Worth quoting

“A passkey is a matched pair of keys, and the two of them do very different jobs. One key is private. It lives on your device and it never leaves.” – Mirko Peters (01:04)

“Phishing works by getting you to hand over a secret, and a passkey leaves you with no secret to hand over.” – Mirko Peters (04:00)

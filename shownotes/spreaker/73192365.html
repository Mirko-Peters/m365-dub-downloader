Why do so many enterprise AI projects never leave the pilot stage? Five-time Microsoft MVP Leon Gordon explains for data leaders and Fabric teams how value-first proofs, governance by design and strong semantic models turn AI demos into production systems.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Introduction and Leon's background
  • 05:11 – Why enterprise AI pilots fail
  • 12:48 – Why Microsoft Fabric matters
  • 25:18 – Fabric in an AI-first world and metadata
  • 29:39 – Semantic models and governance by design
  • 33:01 – Good governance, FabOps and data quality
  • 46:24 – Copilot, Foundry and AI harnesses
  • 50:23 – Business value, future roles and quickfire

Key takeaways

  • Most generative AI pilots fail because they do not start from measurable business value and a governed, AI-ready data foundation.
  • Microsoft Fabric unifies pipelines, notebooks, semantic models, real-time intelligence and apps on one platform, with the semantic model becoming the key context layer for agents.
  • Governance should be baked into the development life cycle from day one, including data quality rules, quarantining anomalies and cost attribution per domain.

Worth quoting

“The vast majority of it comes down to actually not leading with value first, understanding what the return investment is and what the outcome you're trying to deliver is” – Leon Gordon (06:36)

“how we do this is to bake in governance as part of the whole development life cycle so how do we get to an end working goal in the quickest possible way” – Leon Gordon (32:20)

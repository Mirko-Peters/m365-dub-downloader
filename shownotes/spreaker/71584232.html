Your Microsoft 365 tenant changes faster than any review board can check it. This episode is for architects and admins who want to move from manual governance to a self-healing remediation engine that detects and fixes drift automatically, without collapsing under Graph throttling.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why manual review breaks
  • 00:47 – Governance as architecture debt
  • 03:11 – From controls to a self-healing system
  • 06:20 – Failure mode 1: Copilot oversharing
  • 09:19 – Failure mode 2: Teams and private channel sprawl
  • 12:08 – Graph throttling at scale
  • 15:28 – Blueprint: building the remediation engine

Key takeaways

  • Governance written as documentation cannot keep up with a changing tenant; the metric that matters is how long drift stays alive before it is fixed.
  • A self-healing architecture separates signals, desired state, orchestration, enforcement and audit, and is tested by failures like Copilot oversharing and Teams sprawl.
  • Build the remediation engine on Microsoft Graph, Logic Apps and managed identity, with queues, back-off with jitter and pagination so throttling does not break it.

Worth quoting

“Define the state you want, watch for drift from that state, decide what the drift means, then take action automatically.” – Mirko Peters (03:22)

“Rules without runtime enforcement become backlog. Reviews without correction become theater, and human oversight, no matter how smart the people are, loses the race once creation speed exceeds review speed.” – Mirko Peters (02:58)

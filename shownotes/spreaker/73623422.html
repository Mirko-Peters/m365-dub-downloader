Attackers start with what your company exposes publicly: old test sites, forgotten subdomains and services nobody remembers. This episode is for security teams and IT admins who need to see their organization from the outside. You learn how Microsoft Defender EASM discovers internet-facing assets, how to prioritize findings by ownership and risk, and how it fits into the wider Microsoft security stack.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – What strangers can see about your company
  • 00:56 – You can't lock what you can't see
  • 03:29 – What Microsoft Defender EASM actually is
  • 06:05 – How discovery works: from one seed to a map
  • 09:34 – From inventory to action
  • 13:28 – How EASM fits into Microsoft security
  • 16:15 – A simple starting plan

Key takeaways

  • Defender EASM gives an outside-in view of everything your company exposes on the public internet, from forgotten subdomains to expiring certificates.
  • Discovery starts from trusted seeds such as domains, IP ranges and ASNs and follows public connections outward, but your team still has to confirm which assets are really yours.
  • Findings become useful when each asset gets an owner, a business reason and a decision, and when EASM data flows into tools like Log Analytics, Microsoft Sentinel and Exposure Management.

Worth quoting

“Most security tools start from inside your company and protect known devices, known users, and known files. An attacker starts outside, they don't need your company map or a login.” – Mirko Peters (00:28)

“Shadow IT means a tool, service, or system that operates outside the normal company list. It doesn't always come from bad choices. Often someone simply needed to get work done quickly.” – Mirko Peters (03:14)

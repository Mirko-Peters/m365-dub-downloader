Most people use Copilot as a smart stranger that forgets everything after each chat. This episode is for Microsoft 365 architects and power users who want to turn Copilot into a Jarvis-style system with persistent memory, skills, Copilot Cowork, agent flows, Graph grounding and governance, and who need a realistic build plan.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Copilot fails at agency
  • 04:17 – The Jarvis model and persistent memory
  • 12:02 – Copilot Cowork and skills
  • 18:29 – Agent flows and governance
  • 26:38 – Microsoft Graph and grounding
  • 34:43 – Proactive triggers, multi-agent orchestration and Work IQ
  • 45:22 – Building it and avoiding failure modes
  • 56:04 – Trust, real costs and what comes next

Key takeaways

  • Copilot becomes an operating system only when memory, action, reasoning and governance are layered into one architecture.
  • Reusable skill files, Copilot Cowork and agent flows turn one-off prompts into orchestrated, governed workflows grounded in Microsoft Graph data.
  • Start with one high-friction workflow, test against real historical cases, keep humans in the loop and budget for engineering, not just licenses.

Worth quoting

“Real agency isn't about writing better prompts. It starts with structure. It requires a system that actually knows who you are, how you work, and what matters to your business.” – Mirko Peters (00:23)

“An action layer without memory is dangerous because you end up with a system that can do things without knowing when it should.” – Mirko Peters (05:52)

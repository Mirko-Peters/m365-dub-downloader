Your ERP shows every order with a date, yet the shop floor knows by Monday morning that the plan will not survive the week. This episode explains why ERP cannot build an optimal production schedule, what finite scheduling and APS add, and where Microsoft data platforms and AI fit. For manufacturing planners, IT and operations leaders who want schedules people actually trust.

Key takeaways

  • ERP plans material and dates against infinite capacity, so its dates are signals, not executable machine slots.
  • A feasible schedule must reserve real time on machines, tools and people, protect the bottleneck and account for sequence-dependent setups.
  • APS should own detailed scheduling with explicit rules and explanations, while Azure, Fabric and Power BI connect and analyze the data and AI supports rather than decides.

Worth quoting

“So infinite capacity isn't a defect and it isn't an attempt to mislead anyone. It's a boundary around what the planning logic is trying to do.” – Mirko Peters (09:58)

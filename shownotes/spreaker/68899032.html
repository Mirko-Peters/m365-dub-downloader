Quick Adaptive Card Extensions in Teams and Viva Connections look harmless in demos but turn dashboards into a graveyard of orphaned, unlabeled cards. This episode is for Microsoft 365 architects, SharePoint and SPFx developers and governance owners who need a reference architecture, a decision tree and a checklist to approve or block card requests without becoming the villain.

  • 17:02 – The decision tree: block or allow
  • 21:17 – The governance checklist
  • 24:04 – The one rule: treat the ACE as a skin

Key takeaways

  • Adaptive Card Extensions are a distribution layer, not an application, so data, ownership, placement and lifecycle must be governed underneath the card.
  • A governed reference architecture uses Entra ID groups, separate environments, canonical data contracts in SharePoint or Dataverse, Purview labels, CI/CD deployment and telemetry so cards stay disposable.
  • Approve a card only when it reads from a governed store under a standard contract, has two owners, lifecycle dates, a scoped placement and a rollback path; otherwise route it to a real project.

Worth quoting

“The thing most people miss is governance is a workload, not a feature. ASEs don't give you life cycle out of the box. Owners leave, cards keep surfacing.” – Mirko Peters (01:14)

“Treat an ace like a UI skin, not an application. If the data matters, govern the system of record, not the card. If the process matters, push it into a platform with environments, ALM and policy enforcement.” – Mirko Peters (03:39)

Traditional tests assume everything works, but production rarely does. In this episode Mirko Peters explains Azure Chaos Studio for cloud architects, DevOps engineers and SREs, showing how controlled failure experiments reveal weaknesses before your customers do.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Intro: why traditional testing isn't enough
  • 01:28 – What chaos engineering actually is
  • 02:30 – Introducing Azure Chaos Studio
  • 04:10 – Building blocks: targets, steps, branches, actions
  • 06:12 – Running your first chaos experiment
  • 08:08 – Safety first
  • 10:12 – Shifting left, shifting right and game days
  • 12:04 – The real value: building confidence

Key takeaways

  • Chaos engineering injects real, controlled failures to test a hypothesis about how your system survives them.
  • Azure Chaos Studio runs service-direct and agent-based faults through experiments built from targets, steps, branches and actions.
  • Start small in non-production, use managed identities and fault enablement as safety nets, and make experiments a continuous practice.

Worth quoting

“Chaos engineering is a practical method. You inject real controlled failures into your system and observe how it reacts. This doesn't happen in a lab or a simulation. It happens on your actual resources.” – Mirko Peters (01:37)

“Here's the thing, chaos engineering is not random destruction. It's not a developer walking into the server room and pulling cables for fun. It's scientific, repeatable, and safety constrained.” – Mirko Peters (02:02)

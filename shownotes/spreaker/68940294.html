Active Directory quietly turns into a security black hole as exceptions, legacy protocols and forgotten privileges pile up. This episode is for Windows and identity administrators and security teams who want to understand how attackers follow that drift through Kerberos, NTLM and delegation, and which baselines, tiering and detection practices keep AD in a stable orbit.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The universe of Windows infrastructure
  • 09:11 – Critical services: DNS, DHCP, AD DS, SMB, print and Group Policy
  • 15:37 – Threat actors, network mapping and AD enumeration
  • 40:25 – Local privilege escalation and LSASS credential protection
  • 56:03 – Time dilation: patches and technical debt
  • 62:28 – Kerberos, NTLM, trusts and the domain controller as black hole
  • 83:36 – Delegation, service accounts, lateral movement and persistence
  • 106:34 – Detection, response, baselines and the governance checklist

Key takeaways

  • Active Directory rarely fails in one misstep; security drift accumulates through forgotten trusts, temporary GPOs, overprivileged service accounts and unpatched legacy hosts.
  • Kerberos delegation, stale KRBTGT secrets, NTLM relays and shared local admin passwords are the slopes attackers follow, countered by constrained delegation, KRBTGT rotation twice, SMB signing, LDAP channel binding, LAPS and gMSA.
  • Tiered administration with PAWs, Credential Guard and LSA protection, Sysmon and security log correlation, rehearsed forest recovery and named owners keep the directory stable and make drift visible.

Worth quoting

“Security does not fail with noise, it fails with inertia. We add a test trust for a merger that never closes. It remains.” – Mirko Peters (05:10)

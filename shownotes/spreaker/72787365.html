Most Bicep files are just portal clicks translated into code, which is automation, not architecture. This episode is for Azure architects and platform teams and shows how to use Bicep, deployment stacks, management groups, Azure Policy and Azure Verified Modules to build governed, predictable infrastructure that survives organizational change.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Bicep is more than ARM with better syntax
  • 03:06 – What changed in 2026
  • 06:13 – The scope problem
  • 09:33 – Parameters, variables and modules as contracts
  • 19:02 – Deployment stacks and state management
  • 23:02 – Governance at the tenant level: policy, RBAC and AVM
  • 43:22 – Landing zones, environments and pipelines
  • 58:32 – Bicep versus Terraform, drift and brownfield

Key takeaways

  • Enterprise Bicep is about lifecycle, ownership and governance: scope above subscriptions, contracts enforced in parameters and modules, and state managed through deployment stacks.
  • Management groups, Azure Policy and role assignments defined in Bicep let governance cascade automatically to every subscription and landing zone.
  • Build on Azure Verified Modules, publish versioned modules to a registry, deploy through gated pipelines and validate continuously against drift.

Worth quoting

“A module isn't just a reusable template. That's a technical description. A module is a contract. It says, this is how we do networking in our organization.” – Mirko Peters (12:31)

“When you write bicep at management groups scope, you're not deploying resources anymore. You're defining the rules that govern how resources can be deployed.” – Mirko Peters (08:13)

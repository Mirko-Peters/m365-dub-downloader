AI is reshaping both attacks and defenses: how do you embrace it without opening new risks? Mirko talks with Microsoft MVP and CISO Mourtaza Fazlehoussen about Security Copilot, AI governance, Zero Trust for AI and the first hour of a ransomware incident, giving security and IT leaders practical guidance before deploying AI.

  • 31:15 – AI governance and AI policies
  • 39:55 – Zero Trust in the age of AI
  • 50:26 – The first hour of a ransomware attack
  • 55:55 – Autonomous SOCs and rapid fire

Key takeaways

  • Microsoft Security Copilot helps SOC analysts triage, correlate and summarize incidents faster, but it does not replace security architecture, controls or human decisions.
  • AI governance needs concrete policies on approved AI tools, data classification and responsible prompting, supported by Microsoft Purview, Entra and Defender.
  • Zero Trust principles of verify explicitly, least privilege and assume breach apply directly to AI agents and Copilot, and incident readiness must include business continuity planning.

Worth quoting

“Microsoft Security Co-Pilot is a powerful tool. However, it remains a security assistant, but it's not a complete, I would say, a security solution. It helps you to augment your security team.” – Mourtaza Fazlehoussen (17:37)

“if I buy security copilot, then I am safe. Like, you know, it will protect entirely my company, which is not true.” – Mourtaza Fazlehoussen (27:56)

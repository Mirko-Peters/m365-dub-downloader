Many organizations believe their backups make them disaster-proof, until a site outage shows how long a restore really takes. This episode explains Azure Site Recovery in plain English for IT admins and decision makers: how replication, failover and failback work, why ASR is not backup, and when the cost is worth it.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why disaster recovery matters
  • 01:49 – What Azure Site Recovery is
  • 03:20 – How replication works
  • 05:23 – Failover and failback
  • 07:13 – ASR versus Azure Backup
  • 09:48 – When to use ASR
  • 11:50 – Pitfalls and best practices

Key takeaways

  • Azure Site Recovery continuously replicates entire servers to another region so workloads can fail over in minutes rather than being restored over hours or days.
  • ASR does not replace backup: it replicates ransomware and corruption too, so mission-critical workloads need both backup for data and ASR for continuity.
  • Replicate only workloads where downtime has real financial impact, use recovery plans for dependencies and DNS, and run test failovers regularly.

Worth quoting

“Disaster recovery focuses on keeping your business running not just saving your files. It's about business continuity, not data preservation. Backup saves your data, while disaster recovery saves your operations and those are two very different things.” – Mirko Peters (01:32)

“ASR does not protect against ransomware or data corruption. It replicates whatever is on the disk including infections.” – Mirko Peters (04:55)

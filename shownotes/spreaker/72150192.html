Your Power Automate flows keep breaking because they expect perfect data in a world full of typos, PDFs and chat messages. This episode is for Power Platform makers and architects who want to move from brittle if-then logic to probabilistic design with confidence scores, fuzzy matching and self-correcting workflows that survive real enterprise data.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why rigid if-then automation breaks
  • 03:21 – Confidence scores and the green, yellow and red zones
  • 06:43 – Fuzzy matching and semantic logic
  • 08:23 – Self-correcting workflows and dual-path validation
  • 11:45 – The human-in-the-loop reality
  • 13:59 – The agentic enterprise
  • 15:40 – Implementation challenge: add a confidence threshold

Key takeaways

  • Deterministic if-then logic fails on messy, unstructured data, so modern Power Platform designs should route work by confidence score instead of exact matches.
  • Use the LLM for soft reasoning such as extraction, but let deterministic flows or rules engines do the math so hallucinations are caught and recovered from.
  • Treat human review as a designed tier for low-confidence cases, showing reviewers the source, the extraction and the weak field side by side.

Worth quoting

“A confidence score is a value from 0 to 1 that quantifies doubt. It is the models way of saying, "I think this is a match, and here is how much I'm willing to bet on it."” – Mirko Peters (04:12)

“There is one fundamental rule for the modern architect. Never let a large language model do the math. LLMs are engines for soft reasoning, which makes them great at understanding intent or finding a specific detail in a pile of messy text.” – Mirko Peters (09:13)

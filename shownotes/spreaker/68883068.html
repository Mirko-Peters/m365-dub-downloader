Power Automate flows that start as quick wins often end as runaway loops, orphaned approvals and 3 a.m. throttling storms nobody can trace. This episode is for makers, admins and architects who own automation in Microsoft 365 and want the patterns that actually return when called: guarded triggers, try-catch scaffolds, retries with circuit breakers, child flows and a governed orchestrator blueprint.

Key takeaways

  • Triggers are the riskiest part of a flow, so gate them with trigger conditions, pre-filters and server-side queries and keep concurrency off until you own a partition key.
  • Reliable flows follow one scaffold everywhere: try, catch and finally scopes, append-only logging with correlation IDs, named terminations, bounded retries with circuit breakers and a poison queue.
  • Architect automation as a master orchestrator with light task routers and small idempotent child flows, owned by service principals and governed by DLP, environments and regular owner sweeps.

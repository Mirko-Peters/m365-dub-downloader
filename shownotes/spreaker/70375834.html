Many organizations see Power Platform as a tool for hobby apps while manual work, slow approvals and expensive pro-code projects drain their budgets. This episode shows IT leaders, architects and finance stakeholders where the low-code arbitrage lies, from AP automation and compliance evidence to RPA, Dataverse and licensing, and how governance makes it scale safely.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Power Platform as a control plane and the cost of manual entropy
  • 03:47 – Pro-code versus low-code economics
  • 08:07 – The citizen developer factory model
  • 12:07 – Arbitrage plays: forms, AP/AR, compliance, frontline apps and approvals
  • 31:28 – Dataverse, RPA and AI Builder with Copilot
  • 42:48 – Workflow debt, M&A integration kits and licensing arbitrage
  • 55:40 – Governance, measuring ROI and the competitive moat
  • 69:52 – Objections, the transformation path and the closing argument

Key takeaways

  • Power Platform delivers capabilities at a fraction of pro-code cost and in weeks instead of months, which turns manual processes into a measurable arbitrage opportunity.
  • Scaling depends on a citizen developer model backed by zoned governance, DLP policies, RBAC and a Center of Excellence, so speed does not turn into shadow IT.
  • Choosing the right licensing model, such as per flow licensing for shared high-volume processes, and measuring ROI rigorously decide whether the program keeps its funding.

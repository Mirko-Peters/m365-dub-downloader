AI agents that start fast often become slow, costly and unreliable as their prompts grow into huge instruction blobs. This episode shows architects and Copilot Studio makers how modular skills, progressive disclosure and solid governance keep enterprise agents lean, accurate and manageable at scale.

  • 00:46 – Why agents degrade
  • 03:46 – The skill as a reasoning boundary
  • 07:00 – Progressive disclosure and manageability
  • 16:48 – Skills in Copilot Studio and the Dataverse MCP bridge
  • 23:42 – Routing, instructions vs. skills and cost economics
  • 44:13 – Governance: supply chain risk and zoned environments
  • 53:36 – Skill catalogs, measurement and common mistakes

Key takeaways

  • Monolithic system prompts make agents slower, more expensive and less accurate over time, while modular skills loaded through progressive disclosure keep the baseline context lean.
  • A skill's name and description are routing signals for the orchestrator, so they must be precise; universal rules belong in instructions, situational guidance in skills, and each skill should follow one archetype.
  • Skills that bundle scripts are a supply chain risk and need zoned governance, DLP, review, sandboxing and measurement, with Copilot Studio, Dataverse and MCP enabling managed, reusable skill libraries.

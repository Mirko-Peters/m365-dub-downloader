When a critical machine goes down, ERP may still show capacity that no longer exists while the shop floor improvises. This episode guides production planners, plant managers and IT/OT architects from disruption detection and impact tracing to finite-capacity rescheduling, so the factory gets a revised plan it can actually execute.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Intro: a machine outage is a planning event
  • 01:07 – Separating real disruption from shop floor noise
  • 06:57 – How long is the capacity gone?
  • 13:03 – Production context and affected orders
  • 25:51 – Bottlenecks, priorities and constraints
  • 45:16 – Response options and finite capacity rescheduling
  • 75:27 – Information flow from machine to planner
  • 88:00 – Microsoft Fabric, Power BI and automation boundaries

Key takeaways

  • Only a confirmed loss of capacity, judged by expected loss, recovery window and exposed orders, should trigger re-planning, not every PLC signal.
  • Planners need a repair range with a confidence level, an impact list that explains dependencies, and a small set of response options tested with finite capacity.
  • Fabric and Power BI provide governed facts and visibility, while scheduling logic proposes options and people approve the changes that reach the shop floor.

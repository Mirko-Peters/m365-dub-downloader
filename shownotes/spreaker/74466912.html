Business Central covers standard processes, but sooner or later a company needs an extra field, approval step or business rule. In this Knowledge Nugget Mirko Peters explains for beginners and functional consultants how AL and extensions customize Business Central safely without touching the base application.

  • 06:39 – The main AL building blocks
  • 11:04 – Events, triggers and business rules
  • 14:59 – Where AL development happens
  • 17:34 – Example: a customer rewards add-on
  • 18:55 – Your AL development starting point

Key takeaways

  • AL is Microsoft's language for Business Central, and extensions keep custom work separate from the base application so updates do not turn into major rebuilds.
  • Tables, pages, table and page extensions, codeunits, triggers and event subscribers each have one job, and shared business rules belong in a codeunit.
  • AL development happens in Visual Studio Code with the AL language extension, testing belongs in a sandbox, and Git keeps a history of every change.

Worth quoting

“Instead of rewriting the standard application, developers build a separate add-on that connects to it, keeping the standard part in place while the company's own changes sit beside it” – Mirko Peters (01:44)

“changing something just because it looks different from an old system creates extra work later, so good custom work starts with a clear business reason.” – Mirko Peters (02:48)

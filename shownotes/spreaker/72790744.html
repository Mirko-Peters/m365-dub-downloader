DevOps gave developers cloud autonomy but also buried them in networking, security, policy and approval queues. This episode shows Azure platform teams, architects and engineering leaders how platform engineering, golden paths, Bicep modules and automated governance remove that cognitive load and turn ops from gatekeeper into enabler.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why DevOps failed at scale and the cognitive load crisis
  • 07:18 – What platform engineering is and the golden path concept
  • 14:00 – Self-service and internal developer platforms
  • 21:30 – Azure Bicep, modules as products and Azure Verified Modules
  • 32:35 – Policy as code and the shift from tickets to templates
  • 39:40 – Measuring cognitive load, the business case and adoption
  • 48:35 – Bicep modules in governance, Bicep vs Terraform and registries
  • 58:20 – CI/CD automation, gatekeeper to enabler and platform ROI

Key takeaways

  • DevOps at enterprise scale shifted infrastructure complexity onto developers, and platform engineering fixes this by treating infrastructure as a product developers consume through golden paths.
  • Azure Bicep modules, Azure Verified Modules, Azure Policy as code, versioned registries and CI/CD pipelines encode security and compliance so the right way becomes the only easy way.
  • Platform teams must run their internal developer platform as a product, earning adoption rather than forcing it and measuring DORA metrics, satisfaction, adoption and cost avoidance together.

Worth quoting

“But then organizations scaled. 50 developers, 100, 500, and the model broke. DevOps promised shared responsibility, but what it actually delivered was distributed complexity.” – Mirko Peters (01:21)

“A golden path is the simplest, most supported way to get a task done. It is not the only way. You still have escape hatches for the weird edge cases.” – Mirko Peters (09:07)

Shared Wi-Fi passwords and repeated VPN logins are still common in small businesses, yet running a traditional certificate infrastructure is often too much work. This episode explains certificates and Microsoft Cloud PKI in plain language and shows IT admins how Intune can deliver, renew and revoke device certificates behind the scenes.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The device access problem
  • 00:46 – Why certificates exist
  • 03:31 – Traditional PKI as a burden
  • 06:27 – What Microsoft Cloud PKI is
  • 09:40 – How a device gets a certificate
  • 13:09 – Where Cloud PKI fits
  • 16:08 – Starting with a small pilot

Key takeaways

  • Device certificates let managed laptops and phones prove they belong without shared Wi-Fi or VPN passwords.
  • Microsoft Cloud PKI moves the root and issuing certificate authorities into Intune, removing on-premises servers, NDES connectors and HSM hardware from the plan.
  • Cloud PKI only covers Intune-managed devices, so start with one use case, make sure the Wi-Fi, VPN or app trusts the certificate chain, and test renewal and revocation.

Worth quoting

“A shared Wi-Fi password can travel. Someone can write it down forward it, reuse it on a personal device, or keep it after leaving the company. A device certificate gives each managed laptop or phone its own proof instead.” – Mirko Peters (01:56)

“Microsoft Cloud PKI keeps the certificate process, but shifts much of that machinery out of your server room and into the Microsoft Cloud.” – Mirko Peters (06:20)

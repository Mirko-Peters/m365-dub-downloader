Running a Microsoft-first MSP means turning Intune, Defender and Entra ID into a repeatable security service across many customers. Intune MVP Albin Klinaku shares what separates good from great deployments, which Defender and Conditional Access features are often missed, and why phishing-resistant MFA and token protection matter now. A practical episode for MSP owners, IT admins and security architects.

  • 17:57 – Device lifecycle, Autopilot and device preparation
  • 20:50 – Defender: ASR rules, alert fatigue and threat hunting
  • 30:32 – Vulnerability and patch management
  • 32:45 – Entra ID, Conditional Access and phishing-resistant MFA
  • 43:40 – Automation, metrics, Security Copilot and security culture

Key takeaways

  • A great Intune deployment applies security baselines to all devices, blocks unwanted enrollment such as personal devices, and is built with a cloud-native mindset rather than copying the old MDM.
  • In Defender, attack surface reduction rules should move from audit to block mode after reviewing the audit logs, and patch management must be in place before vulnerability management makes sense.
  • For identities, organizations should move to phishing-resistant authentication with passkeys or Windows Hello for Business and make full use of Conditional Access features such as risk-based policies and token protection.

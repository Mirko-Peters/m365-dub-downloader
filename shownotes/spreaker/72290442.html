Many organizations ask Copilot to forecast sales or churn and get confident but wrong answers. This episode shows BI teams, data scientists and Fabric architects how to ground Copilot in Power BI semantic models, semantic link and Fabric data agents so that predictions become accurate, secure and auditable.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Copilot fails at prediction
  • 03:58 – Why natural language queries fail at prediction
  • 07:05 – What Power BI semantic models really are and why metadata matters
  • 13:20 – Semantic link and SemPy as the bridge to code
  • 20:10 – The architecture: Copilot orchestrates, models predict
  • 29:50 – RLS, OLS and the prepped-for-AI certification
  • 39:55 – Semantic Link Labs, data pipeline and Copilot Studio agents
  • 52:20 – Grounding, monitoring, common mistakes and next steps

Key takeaways

  • Copilot cannot calculate or forecast on its own; reliable predictions come from models built in Fabric notebooks, while Copilot interprets the question and explains the result.
  • Building predictive models on semantic model measures through semantic link keeps definitions, relationships and row-level security identical between reports and predictions.
  • Rich metadata, tested RLS and OLS, a curated AI schema and the prepped-for-AI checklist are the foundation that keeps Copilot grounded and prevents sensitive data from leaking.

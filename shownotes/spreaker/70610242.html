Many organizations enable Power Platform for citizen developers and later find thousands of unmanaged apps, flows and connectors in the default environment. This episode is for architects, IT leaders and governance teams running Power Platform at scale. You will learn why low-code means distributed governance and how to enforce environments, ALM, connector policies and ownership before sprawl turns into risk and cost.

  • 63:28 – The CoE as architecture enforcer and cultural change
  • 85:31 – Governance checklist and executive risk summary

Key takeaways

  • Power Platform is a distributed development platform inside Microsoft 365, not a productivity tool, and treating it like Excel leads to default environment sprawl, risky connectors, flow explosions, licensing surprises and zombie apps.
  • Standard Centers of Excellence often fail because they only observe and advise; governance works when it is enforced architecturally through tiered environments, mandatory ALM pipelines, DLP connector segmentation and service account ownership.
  • Start by locking down the default environment, creating personal, team and enterprise tiers, retiring apps after 90 days without usage and giving the CoE real authority with executive sponsorship.

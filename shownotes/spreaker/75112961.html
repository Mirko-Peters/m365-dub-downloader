Your ERP assigns a finish date, but the machine that must make the parts is already buried under more work than it can finish. This episode explains for manufacturing planners and IT/OT teams how finite capacity scheduling works, from bottlenecks, changeovers and material release to rescheduling, APS and where Microsoft Fabric, Power Platform and Copilot fit.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Introduction: when the plan doesn't fit the machine
  • 01:49 – Infinite capacity planning
  • 05:56 – A factory scenario with one bottleneck
  • 10:23 – What finite actually means and the data model
  • 29:46 – Forward, backward scheduling and sequencing
  • 40:08 – The morning shift decision, changeovers and constraints
  • 73:08 – Breakdowns and rescheduling without chaos
  • 90:08 – Where Microsoft fits, optimization and AI

Key takeaways

  • ERP and MRP plan against infinite capacity, so finite capacity scheduling is needed to test whether work actually fits the usable time of machines, people, tools and material.
  • Sequencing and changeovers are policy decisions with trade-offs between due dates, setups and downstream flow, and a good schedule makes those consequences visible to the planner.
  • Microsoft Fabric, Power BI, Power Platform and Copilot support data, exceptions and explanations, but the scheduling logic and plant-specific rules must live in an engine such as an APS system that the plant owns.

Worth quoting

“Finite capacity scheduling doesn't create capacity. It makes the competition for capacity visible while you still have time to choose.” – Mirko Peters (14:04)

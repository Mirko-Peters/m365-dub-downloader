Legacy Power Apps portals look stable because they are quiet, but their Liquid templates and JavaScript injections hide business logic from security teams and audits. This episode helps Power Platform owners and IT leaders see the governance, talent and AI-readiness costs of waiting, and why moving to a Dataverse-native Power Pages model stops the budget bleed.

  • 00:34 – The governance black hole: shadow logic in Liquid
  • 03:58 – The JavaScript injection trap
  • 06:51 – 2026 security unification and the end of dual identities
  • 08:18 – Technical debt as a talent and career risk
  • 10:50 – The AI readiness wall
  • 13:39 – Financial post-mortem: TCO of waiting
  • 15:24 – This week's challenge: audit your Liquid templates

Key takeaways

  • Logic hidden in Liquid templates and client-side JavaScript sits outside Dataverse auditing and security roles, so legacy portals create a governance gap that audits will expose.
  • The 2026 Wave 1 changes sync Power Pages contacts and web roles into Dataverse users and security roles, which keeps existing sites running but surfaces every dependency on the old split identity model.
  • Staying on a legacy portal raises total cost of ownership through scarce specialist talent and blocks AI agents, which need governed, API-accessible business rules instead of visual hacks.

Worth quoting

“Those injections are client side vulnerabilities that expose your business rules to the browser. Every piece of logic you write in JavaScript is essentially a suggestion, not a rule.” – Mirko Peters (04:16)

“Modern governance requires a single source of truth. If your security team runs a report on who can access Table X, they expect the answer to be in dataverse.” – Mirko Peters (02:34)

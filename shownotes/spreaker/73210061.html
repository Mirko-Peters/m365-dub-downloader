Microsoft Purview is already in your tenant, and that ease is exactly why so many data governance rollouts end in permission sprawl and an unused catalog. This episode shows data governance and platform teams how to roll out Purview use case first, with a four-layer permission blueprint, role groups, data products and PIM-protected admins.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Purview's ease becomes a trap
  • 03:10 – Big bang versus use case first
  • 06:28 – The four-layer permission blueprint
  • 20:52 – Five role groups and business-aligned collections
  • 32:37 – Data products: where structure becomes value
  • 36:09 – The medallion accountability chain
  • 39:38 – Caging the Purview administrator with PIM
  • 42:41 – Rollout sequence and failure modes

Key takeaways

  • Start a Microsoft Purview rollout with one concrete business question instead of scanning and cataloging everything.
  • Purview access spans four layers (tenant, data map, unified catalog, governance domain), so bundle each persona's permissions into about five role groups assigned to Entra groups.
  • Protect the Purview administrator role with just-in-time PIM activation and make ownership explicit for every data product and medallion layer.

Worth quoting

“governance isn't technology, governance is structure. It's the deliberate choices you make about ownership, accountability and how information flows. Those choices have to come first. The tool is the byproduct.” – Mirko Peters (02:30)

“The moment you decide to catalog everything you've already failed, not because you didn't work hard enough, but because of your intent you chose to build for completeness, instead of building for a person” – Mirko Peters (03:36)

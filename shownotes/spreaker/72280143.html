Why do most enterprise AI pilots in Microsoft 365 stall, even when the technology works? Drawing on a simulation of 100 synthetic companies, this episode maps five predictable governance failure patterns for IT leaders and architects preparing for Copilot and AI agents. You get readiness frameworks, an operating model and a 90-day assessment to fix the foundation before you scale.

Key takeaways

  • AI does not create new governance problems in Microsoft 365; it surfaces existing identity blind spots, abandoned workspaces, ungoverned automations and paper-only controls at conversational speed.
  • Clear, singular accountability for AI governance and disciplined measurement were the strongest predictors of adoption success in the simulation, ahead of budget and tools.
  • Sequence the work: identity, then data, then collaboration, then automation, and only then introduce Copilot and agents, starting with read-only scenarios and a 90-day readiness assessment.

Worth quoting

“Governance isn't a document. It's a behavioral system. It's the set of constraints, incentives, and feedback loops that shape how people actually work inside your organization.” – Mirko Peters (01:33)

“A colleague asks a question, the AI surfaces an answer that reveals proprietary information to someone who shouldn't see it. Now you have an incident, now you have a compliance violation. That's not an AI problem, that's a governance problem that AI amplified.” – Mirko Peters (01:58)

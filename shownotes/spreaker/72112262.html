Active Directory is far from dead, and a single compromised device can still hand attackers the whole domain. In this conversation for IT admins and security teams, Viktor Hedberg explains AD tiering, privileged access workstations and why getting the basics right beats buying another security tool.

Key takeaways

  • Administrative tiering boxes domain controllers, Entra Connect, PKI and similar systems into tier zero and blocks high-privilege accounts from logging on to lower tiers, limiting credential exposure.
  • Privileged access workstations combined with authentication policy silos and remote administration tools mean admin credentials are only usable on one device and are not left behind on servers.
  • Get the security basics right first, such as blocking domain admins from logging on everywhere, before adding PAM, PIM or other tools, and keep on-prem administration independent of the cloud.

Worth quoting

“So the concept of administrative tiering is in a nutshell. We box things into different administrative zones to prevent those nasty escalations or lateral movements from within Active Directory itself.” – Viktor Hedberg (08:17)

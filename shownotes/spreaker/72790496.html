Manual governance with PDFs, spreadsheets and change boards cannot keep pace with pipelines that deploy in minutes, so drift and over-privileged identities pile up unnoticed. This episode is for Azure architects and security engineers, and lays out a complete blueprint with Azure Policy, RBAC, PIM, managed identities, Key Vault and Entra ID Governance to make compliance automatic, auditable and developer friendly.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The gap between governance speed and deployment speed
  • 03:48 – RBAC versus Azure Policy
  • 07:16 – The governance stack, management groups and landing zones
  • 10:45 – Azure Policy effects, initiatives, safe rollout and policy as code
  • 19:07 – RBAC drift and PIM just-in-time access
  • 27:45 – Service principals, managed identities and Key Vault
  • 43:00 – Developer self-service and golden paths
  • 49:50 – Entra ID Governance, Purview, Zero Trust, monitoring and roadmap

Key takeaways

  • RBAC decides who can act while Azure Policy decides what may exist, and governance at scale needs both, layered with identity governance, resource locks and monitoring through a management group hierarchy.
  • Policies should roll out audit first, then modify or deploy-if-not-exists, then deny, ring by ring and managed as code in Git with pull requests and pipelines.
  • Group-based RBAC, PIM, managed identities, Key Vault with RBAC authorization and purge protection, golden paths and Entra ID Governance turn compliance into a continuous, self-enforcing system aligned with Zero Trust.

Worth quoting

“Policies exist on paper, they're reviewed annually, they're signed and filed, but they're never technically enforced. A policy that lives only in a document is not a control, it's a wish.” – Mirko Peters (03:08)

“RBAC governs the actor, policy governs the environment. One without the other leaves you exposed in ways you won't see until something breaks.” – Mirko Peters (05:32)

Your Azure landing zone may be slowing teams down so much that they build around it. This episode is for cloud architects and platform teams running Azure at scale, and shows how Bicep modules, subscription vending and guardrail-style governance turn the official path into the fastest path.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why rigid landing zones create shadow IT
  • 03:42 – Subscription strategy and subscription vending
  • 08:37 – Network architecture and the connectivity tax
  • 12:19 – Shared services and multi-region design
  • 19:42 – Bicep modules as infrastructure contracts
  • 35:42 – Policy as code, identity and observability
  • 48:01 – Cost governance, security baselines and shadow IT signals
  • 59:43 – Platform engineering, KPIs and designing for evolution

Key takeaways

  • Landing zone code encodes organizational decision rights, and gatekeeper governance that slows provisioning pushes teams into shadow IT.
  • Subscription vending, versioned Bicep modules consumed by pinned version, and audit-first policy rollout make the governed path faster than the ungoverned one.
  • Treat the landing zone as a versioned platform product: centralize what must be uniform, distribute what must move fast, and measure provisioning time, compliance trends and drift.

Worth quoting

“Every decision encoded in those bicep templates is actually a decision about who gets to decide things. Who can create a resource? Who approves policy changes? How fast can a team get what they need?” – Mirko Peters (01:41)

“Gatekeeper governance designed to reduce risk actually increases it. Because the more friction you create in the official path, the more teams find unofficial paths.” – Mirko Peters (02:27)

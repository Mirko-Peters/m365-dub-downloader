Your org chart, governance model and adoption dashboards may describe an organization that no longer exists. This episode is for IT leaders, Microsoft 365 architects and change practitioners who see high usage but slow, heavy work. Learn how to read Teams, SharePoint and permission signals to find the real organization and fix it before Copilot amplifies the gaps.

  • 16:28 – Signal two: SharePoint and OneDrive reveal how knowledge moves
  • 24:02 – Signal three: identity and permission drift define the real org
  • 35:00 – Case study: the well-structured, slow organization
  • 47:56 – Decision latency, rework, shadow systems and why Copilot becomes a mirror
  • 73:10 – Four steps: map decisions, trace data, find hidden owners, simplify

Key takeaways

  • High Teams activity, duplicated files in SharePoint and OneDrive, and drifting permissions are signals of structural compensation, not proof of collaboration maturity.
  • Copilot reasons over the content and access your environment actually contains, so fragmented knowledge and permission drift turn it into a mirror of organizational problems rather than a fix.
  • Start by mapping one real decision end to end, tracing its files and versions, identifying hidden owners and measuring friction, then simplify the environment before scaling it.

Worth quoting

“Teams is not just a collaboration tool. It is a coordination layer. Coordination layers become noisy when the underlying organization is fragmented.” – Mirko Peters (09:09)

“Decision latency is hard to see because it accumulates in the gaps between visible actions like the space between a message and an answer or the time between a decision and the final version.” – Mirko Peters (15:13)

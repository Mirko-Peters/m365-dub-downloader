Most Dataverse security problems start when access is granted one ticket at a time instead of by structure. This episode walks architects through business units, security roles, teams, ownership, column and hierarchy security, and sharing, and shows how to design a model that survives reorgs, audits and Copilot.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The question Dataverse security actually answers
  • 03:11 – The four layers and the environment boundary
  • 08:34 – Business units and why trees break at scale
  • 18:15 – Security roles, privileges and access depth
  • 25:10 – Record ownership and teams
  • 37:15 – Column-level security and hierarchy security
  • 51:45 – Sharing, its cost and how access is evaluated
  • 64:01 – Role and team design, anti-patterns and governance

Key takeaways

  • Dataverse security decides which rows a user can reach by combining identity, privilege, scope and record ownership, and access is always additive.
  • Business units should model real data boundaries such as legal, regulatory or contractual walls, not the org chart, and teams should own long-lived data.
  • Sharing, organization depth and one-off roles are shortcuts that erode the model, so roles, teams and profiles belong in solutions with reviews and auditing.

Worth quoting

“A Dataverse security model only scales when it's designed around ownership and hierarchy, not around individual access requests.” – Mirko Peters (00:12)

“So treat organization depth the way you treat a master key. A small number of people need it admins, integration accounts, maybe a data steward or two.” – Mirko Peters (24:28)

Brand colors in a Power BI theme can quietly bury alerts, erase subtotals and make slicer states impossible to read. This episode is for report creators and BI teams who want trustworthy dashboards, and it walks through five invisible theme failures plus a pass/fail validation protocol with contrast gates, JSON locks and organizational themes.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Power BI themes can lie
  • 00:42 – Alert contrast and redundancy
  • 05:29 – Matrix subtotals in disguise
  • 10:03 – Tooltips: context lost on hover
  • 14:50 – Card visuals: fonts and hierarchy
  • 19:45 – Slicer state deception
  • 23:47 – Validation protocol, automation and version control

Key takeaways

  • Power BI themes are a governance layer: low contrast and undefined styles can hide alerts, subtotals, tooltips, card hierarchy and slicer states.
  • Define alert states, subtotal styles, tooltip elements, card ratios and all four slicer states explicitly in the theme JSON and test them against real backgrounds with pass/fail contrast thresholds.
  • Manage themes like code: validation report, version control with pull requests, signed organizational themes and blocked local overrides on certified workspaces.

Worth quoting

“Never color only. If an alert state is critical, bind an icon, an adjacent label or a pattern.” – Mirko Peters (02:13)

“For text and UI labels, enforce a 4.51 contrast ratio minimum. For graphical marks, bars, lines, points, never under 3-1.” – Mirko Peters (01:18)

Many small businesses assume Azure's built-in defenses keep their websites and APIs safe from DDoS attacks, yet moderate attacks can still take a single workload offline. This episode explains in plain English what DDoS attacks are, what the free tier covers, how Azure DDoS Protection and WAF fill the gaps, and how to build a practical layered defense.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why DDoS attacks matter for anyone online
  • 00:58 – What a DDoS attack actually is
  • 02:56 – The free infrastructure protection
  • 04:44 – Network Protection vs. IP Protection
  • 07:04 – Layer 7 attacks and where WAF fits
  • 10:31 – The largest DDoS attack Azure has handled
  • 11:47 – Why small businesses need to care
  • 14:00 – A layered DDoS defense strategy

Key takeaways

  • Every Azure customer gets free infrastructure-level DDoS protection, but it protects Azure's platform and may not mitigate moderate attacks against your specific workload.
  • Azure DDoS Protection comes as Network Protection, with rapid response support, cost protection and a WAF discount, or as the cheaper per-IP IP Protection, both adding adaptive tuning, metrics and logging.
  • Because DDoS protection only covers layers 3 and 4, a Web Application Firewall with the HTTP DDoS rule set, rate limiting and bot protection is needed to stop layer 7 attacks.

Worth quoting

“If a moderate attack hits your specific application, the free tier might not step in. From Azure's perspective, the traffic isn't enough to threaten the platform, so it doesn't trigger any mitigation.” – Mirko Peters (04:16)

“Azure DDoS protection only covers layers 3 and 4, the network and transport layers. It looks at IP addresses, ports, protocols and packet rates, but it never inspects the actual content of your HTTP requests.” – Mirko Peters (07:08)

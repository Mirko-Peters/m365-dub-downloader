Many organizations build in Azure first and think about governance only when security incidents or exploding costs force them to. Azure MVP and MCT Vladimir Stefanovic shows cloud architects and IT leaders how early decisions on landing zones, policies, naming and permissions prevent expensive redesigns later.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Meet Vladimir Stefanovic: from local IT to Azure MVP
  • 04:50 – Why governance must start on day zero
  • 10:40 – Selling governance to the C-level: security and cost
  • 16:45 – Good and bad Azure networking decisions
  • 21:10 – CAF, WAF and policy-driven governance
  • 27:25 – Costly governance mistakes, permissions and automation
  • 36:20 – Naming conventions, maturity and Zero Trust
  • 43:30 – AI, Azure complexity and T-shaped engineers

Key takeaways

  • Governance, landing zones and permissions need to be planned from day one, because redesigning a fast-growing production environment later is far harder and rarely gets prioritized.
  • Security incidents and rising costs are the triggers that most often convince management to invest in governance, so frame proposals around those two risks.
  • Keep it simple: a small set of well-chosen policies, a proper naming convention and automation-first deployments cover most of what matters, while experience prevents over-engineering.

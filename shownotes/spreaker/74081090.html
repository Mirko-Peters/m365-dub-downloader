Data rarely stays in one app: it is copied, exported, renamed and forgotten across Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Fabric. This episode is for Microsoft 365 admins, data owners and compliance teams who need a plain-English map of Microsoft Purview. You learn how its data map, catalog, labels, DLP and investigation tools fit together to make data findable, trustworthy and protected.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why data sprawl turns into business risk
  • 04:39 – What Microsoft Purview actually is
  • 08:44 – Find, name and trust data: data map, catalog and lineage
  • 12:13 – Classify and protect: sensitivity labels and DLP
  • 16:18 – Manage risk, records and investigations
  • 19:47 – How the pieces work together

Key takeaways

  • Microsoft Purview does not store your data; it adds rules, context and visibility around data that stays in Microsoft 365, Azure, Fabric and supported external sources.
  • The data map, unified catalog, glossary, lineage and data quality checks help people find data, know who owns it and judge whether it can be trusted.
  • Sensitivity labels, DLP, audit, retention, eDiscovery and communication compliance protect information as it moves and give responsible teams records and context when something goes wrong.

Worth quoting

“An accessible report is not always a trusted report. That difference can save a team from building a decision around incomplete or outdated information.” – Mirko Peters (12:09)

“A sensitivity label and DLP work together, but they do different jobs. The label identifies information and describes how it should be handled. DLP checks an action as it happens and can warn the person, block the action or record it for review.” – Mirko Peters (14:43)

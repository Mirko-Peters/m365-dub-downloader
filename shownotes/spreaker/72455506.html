Project Online is being retired, and many project-based organizations still have to decide where their projects, time entries and resource plans will live next. In this conversation Microsoft MVP Joe Griffin explains Dynamics 365 Project Operations, how to migrate from Project Online with the schedule API, and how AI agents and Power Platform change project delivery.

  • 17:14 – Change management and a migration success story
  • 21:28 – AI agents in Project Operations
  • 25:23 – Power Platform, governance, Azure and Foundry
  • 40:03 – Quick-fire round

Key takeaways

  • Project Online retires at the end of September, and Microsoft's three routes are Planner, Project Server or Dynamics 365 Project Operations, which Joe recommends for organizations with time entry and complex work breakdown needs.
  • Large migrations need repeatable scripts against the supported schedule API, plus a deliberate effort to simplify overly complex MPP files and work breakdown structures.
  • AI in Project Operations already drafts time entries, status reports, risks and resource suggestions, while Power Platform, Dataverse, Azure and Foundry extend the platform within supported customization boundaries.

Worth quoting

“if your organization currently relies on project online in a significant way, then you very much need to be starting to have those conversations about how you can manage this transition and move across to an alternative solution.” – Joe Griffin (11:11)

Apps, pipelines and AI agents need access too, yet many still hide passwords in code and config files. In this Simply Explained episode, Mirko Peters gives Azure admins, developers and architects a plain-English map of Microsoft Entra workload identities, managed identities, service principals and federation, plus a practical checklist for securing them.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Intro: who signs in when software needs access?
  • 00:53 – User identities and workload identities
  • 03:19 – App registrations, service principals and managed identities
  • 07:09 – What happens during a token request
  • 10:30 – Security rules for workload identities
  • 14:06 – Picking the right pattern and a workload identity check

Key takeaways

  • Workload identities give applications, automations and AI agents their own Entra identity instead of shared service accounts or passwords in code.
  • An application registration is the app's global design, a service principal is its local identity in each tenant, and a managed identity is a service principal that Azure manages for an Azure resource.
  • Prefer managed identities inside Azure and workload identity federation outside it, use certificates over client secrets, grant minimal permissions and give every workload identity an owner.

Worth quoting

“A workload identity is different. It's a digital badge for software. That software could be an app your team built an automation that runs on a schedule, a background service, or even an AI agent” – Mirko Peters (01:25)

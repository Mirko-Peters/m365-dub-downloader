Microsoft 365 Copilot doesn't arrive in a clean tenant; it inherits years of oversharing, orphaned workspaces, guest links and broken ownership. Microsoft MVP Richard Harbridge of ShareGate joins Mirko Peters to explain why AI readiness is really a Microsoft 365 modernization and governance challenge. IT leaders and admins get practical patterns for measuring sprawl, remediating safely and governing the agents that come next.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Intro: Copilot doesn't arrive in a clean tenant
  • 02:28 – Sediments and the many types of sprawl
  • 07:24 – Workspace lifecycle, expiration and sharing links
  • 17:53 – Distributed governance and agents as admin tools
  • 24:03 – From AI readiness to AI fitness
  • 29:36 – Consolidation and migrations as governance opportunities
  • 37:38 – Governing AI agents
  • 43:48 – Where to start, future of governance and rapid fire

Key takeaways

  • Copilot does not create a new mess but amplifies years of Microsoft 365 sprawl in permissions, ownership, lifecycle, guest access and connectors.
  • Governance should be continuous, distributed and recoverable: notify department leads, phase remediation, keep actions restorable and use agents or tools to scale the work.
  • Instead of a one-time AI readiness checklist, measure sprawl maturity, use migrations and consolidations to clean up, and make sure governance of agents scales with how fast they are created.

Worth quoting

“I don't think anyone has a clean Microsoft 365 environment like we all inherit this mess. I always think of this analogy with sediments.” – Richard Harbridge (02:28)

“So when we say AI readiness, what we really mean is we want to say, what's our AI ambition? What is our AI fitness? And essentially how quickly can we turn it into value or how can we responsibly accelerate it?” – Richard Harbridge (26:07)

Many enterprises put a large language model at the centre of every AI agent and wonder why it fails in production. This conversation with Microsoft AI MVP Karthikeyan VK is for architects and developers building enterprise agents. Learn why orchestration, memory, evaluation and deterministic engineering matter more than a bigger model.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Introduction and Karthikeyan's MVP journey
  • 02:11 – Microsoft Foundry and the Microsoft AI ecosystem
  • 05:20 – Too much LLM: why enterprise agents fail
  • 13:50 – Orchestration, memory and evaluation
  • 21:50 – Multi-agent systems and common architecture mistakes
  • 31:25 – Scalability, observability and responsible AI
  • 39:27 – From proof of concept to production
  • 42:20 – Hype vs. reality, small language models and rapid fire

Key takeaways

  • Use the LLM as the reasoning layer that understands requests and picks tools, while deterministic, self-written workflows and tools do the actual business work.
  • Production-grade agents need orchestration, state and memory management, evaluation loops, observability and clear decision boundaries, which Microsoft Foundry provides largely built in.
  • Specialised small language models and domain-specific agents will outperform general-purpose models in the enterprise on cost, latency and fit, but humans stay in the loop.

Worth quoting

“You need to change the architecture and you'll think in terms of old bad jobs because that is more deterministic rather than probabilistic. A real system cannot have probabilistic answers.” – Karthikeyan (06:39)

“whenever you have an LLM and the LLM's work itself is kind of doing the job, then it becomes a problem. You should use proper tools.” – Karthikeyan (09:04)

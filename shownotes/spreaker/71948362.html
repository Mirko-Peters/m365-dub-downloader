Dynamics 365 Finance and Operations no longer runs as a standalone ERP, and poorly designed integrations quickly become fragile and slow. In this episode, Microsoft MVP and MCT Anitha Eswaran helps architects, developers and consultants choose the right integration patterns and middleware. You learn how to design for volume, failure and observability so your integrations scale with the business.

In this episode

  • 07:13 – Common integration mistakes: volume and frequency
  • 08:25 – Integration architecture and the four pillars
  • 14:00 – Integration patterns: DIXF, OData, custom services, business events, dual-write
  • 20:50 – Middleware, anti-patterns, tools and security
  • 27:00 – Performance, multi-country rollouts and error handling
  • 37:27 – Developer tips, career advice and AI
  • 45:42 – Rapid fire and closing thoughts

Key takeaways

  • Before designing a Dynamics 365 Finance and Operations integration, study volume, frequency and load, because these decide between real-time, near real-time and asynchronous patterns.
  • Each integration pattern has clear trade-offs: DIXF suits bulk data, OData suits small real-time transactions but throttles, custom services need development and business events enable decoupled, event-driven designs.
  • Resilient integrations assume failure, use middleware such as Service Bus, Event Grid and Logic Apps to offload the AOS, and build observability with tracing, alerts and retries.

Worth quoting

“if it is for more than 10,000 per 5 minutes then you need to decide as an asynchronous if it is less than 1000 then you need to see it as synchronous” – Anitha Eswaran (07:43)

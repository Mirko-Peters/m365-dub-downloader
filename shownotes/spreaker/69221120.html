HR platforms promise a clean hire-to-retire lifecycle, yet policy ends up scattered across templates, workflows, Power Automate flows and Entra exceptions until nobody can explain why someone still has access. This episode is for HR, identity and platform architects working with Dynamics 365 HR, Workday, SuccessFactors and Microsoft 365, and it delivers a diagnostic checklist, a reference architecture and a 90-day plan to make HR systems explainable and ready for AI.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – What hire-to-retire actually is
  • 05:19 – Configuration entropy: how setup becomes the system
  • 10:02 – Dynamics 365 HR: transactional core, adaptive debt
  • 14:49 – Workday and SuccessFactors: rigor and local entropy
  • 25:43 – Entra ID as the shadow system of record
  • 31:31 – Power Automate and the debugging economy
  • 36:57 – Why AI pilots fail and the capability-obligation-identity model
  • 50:32 – Reference architecture, governance and the 90-day plan

Key takeaways

  • Hire-to-retire is not a linear process but a stream of facts, obligations and identity changes processed by many systems that make independent decisions.
  • Policy hidden in templates, stages, connector conditions, flows and Conditional Access exceptions creates configuration entropy that AI will mirror instead of fix.
  • A four-layer architecture with a versioned policy layer, immutable events, subscriber-only execution and an explanation-first AI layer lets HR own intent while platforms execute and Entra enforces.

Worth quoting

“AI amplifies drift, it doesn't heal it. Models learn from artifacts. If intent is implicit and scattered, AI infers policy from stale templates, inconsistent labels and noisy histories.” – Mirko Peters (03:12)

“Access is not a derivative of personhood, it's a derivative of risk, obligation and capability, those change outside HR.” – Mirko Peters (26:57)

Most organizations still treat meeting-room audio as an afterthought, even though Copilot now depends on it. In this episode, Microsoft MVP Josh Blalock explains to IT leaders and meeting-room planners how Teams evolved from Skype for Business, why audio beats video, what Teams certification really means and how to plan rooms that give AI clean data.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Welcome and Josh's path from Air Force IT to unified communications
  • 03:39 – From Skype for Business to Microsoft Teams
  • 06:12 – Becoming an MVP and the Shure Ignition program
  • 17:55 – Why audio matters more than video
  • 23:36 – Hardware vs. software: onboard DSPs and cloud audio processing
  • 30:42 – Teams certified devices and why certification matters
  • 34:48 – Planning meeting rooms for challenging acoustics
  • 40:10 – Comms vNext and Josh's next chapter

Key takeaways

  • In AI-enabled meetings, audio is no longer just sound but the real-time data Copilot uses for summaries, action items and documents, so 'good enough' audio limits your AI investment.
  • Clean audio should be produced in the room, for example by onboard software DSPs in microphones and room kits, before Microsoft Teams processes it in the cloud.
  • Teams certified devices pass rigorous Microsoft testing and are the supported option, and complex rooms still benefit from an integrator, a proof of concept and acoustic treatment.

Worth quoting

“If your video fails, it's okay. The meeting can still go on. You can still hear people with audio. If your audio fails, now you're done. Your meeting is over.” – Josh Blalock (19:48)

“It's no longer just spoken words and audio. Now audio is actually real-time data that co-pilot is going to use to do a whole bunch of stuff to build documents, to create task lists, to send emails” – Josh Blalock (20:54)

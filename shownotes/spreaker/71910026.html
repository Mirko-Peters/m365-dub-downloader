Your microservices look healthy, yet capacity is silently collapsing behind one slow dependency. This episode is for .NET architects and engineering leaders who want to understand cascading latency failures. Learn how bulkheads, circuit breakers and controlled degradation keep one slow call from taking down the whole platform.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Silent latency: the real cloud toxin
  • 04:10 – Why retries turn instability into a self-inflicted attack
  • 07:16 – Bulkhead isolation changes the system model
  • 11:00 – Circuit breakers stop panic before it spreads
  • 14:32 – Build for controlled degradation, not perfect uptime
  • 18:10 – Operationalizing resilience in .NET teams

Key takeaways

  • In distributed .NET systems, a slow dependency often does more damage than a crashed one because waiting work quietly consumes threads, sockets and queue capacity.
  • Retries should only be used for brief, random faults; against an overloaded dependency they multiply pressure, so bulkheads and scoped circuit breakers are needed to contain the blast radius.
  • Resilience is a business policy: define service quality tiers, degraded modes and per-dependency rules for timeout, retry, breaker, fallback and isolation, then test them deliberately.

Worth quoting

“A hard failure gets rejected immediately, but a slow failure gets admitted into the system and then multiplied across the entire path.” – Mirko Peters (02:18)

“Every retry you send is just more traffic aimed at a service that already proved it couldn't handle the first request.” – Mirko Peters (04:29)

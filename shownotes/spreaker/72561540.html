If agents can do the work that once required people clicking through screens, the user interface and the per-seat SaaS model lose their foundation. This episode is for IT leaders, enterprise architects and software decision makers planning for agent-driven operations. You learn what agents actually need, where computer-using agents fit, and how to govern, measure and scale them.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The UI was always a workaround and seat-based pricing breaks
  • 06:44 – What agents actually require and the API-first imperative
  • 13:28 – Workflow capital as the real moat
  • 17:23 – Non-human identities and Agent 365 as the control plane
  • 23:00 – Foundry Agent Service and computer-using agents as the bridge
  • 29:44 – Safety, observability, skills, ROI and ownership
  • 43:45 – Operations, incident response and continuous improvement
  • 52:44 – Investment case, strategic choices, roadmap and culture

Key takeaways

  • Agents do not need dashboards; they need stable APIs, explicit business logic, context and governance, which puts seat-based SaaS pricing and UI-first architectures under pressure.
  • Computer-using agents can bridge legacy systems without APIs, but their success rate drops sharply on complex multi-step workflows, so API-first modernization remains the long-term goal.
  • Running agents at scale needs agent identities with zero standing privilege, a control plane such as Agent 365, a runtime such as Foundry Agent Service, clear ownership, kill switches and continuous evaluation.

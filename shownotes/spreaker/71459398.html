Your Microsoft Fabric bill keeps rising even though your data volume barely changed. This episode is for data platform owners and BI leads who need to understand where capacity units really go. You learn how to spot the SQL convenience tax and refresh storms and how to run a 30-day governance reset.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why the Fabric bill climbs while data stays flat
  • 00:36 – The model behind the bill: shared capacity, smoothing and overage
  • 03:00 – Where capacity units actually go
  • 06:01 – Scenario 1: The SQL convenience tax
  • 09:01 – Scenario 2: The invisible refresh storm
  • 12:01 – Why more capacity usually makes it worse
  • 14:25 – The 30-day governance reset

Key takeaways

  • Microsoft Fabric bills a shared capacity pool, so costs follow workload behavior such as query design, refresh overlap and concurrency rather than stored data volume.
  • Using the SQL endpoint for everything and letting teams schedule refreshes independently are two common patterns that drain capacity units.
  • A 30-day reset with workload isolation, SQL guardrails, refresh orchestration, showback and a weekly review of the top consumers works better than buying more capacity.

Worth quoting

“Fabric doesn't bill in silos. It builds the shared pool. So a badly timed refresh, an expensive SQL pattern, or a noisy export process doesn't just hurt that one workload.” – Mirko Peters (01:38)

“High usage during real demand can be healthy. High usage from low value repetition is just waste with a chart attached.” – Mirko Peters (05:47)

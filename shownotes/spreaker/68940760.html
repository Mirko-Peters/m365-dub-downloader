Active Directory rarely fails loudly; it drifts, one exception and one forgotten service account at a time, until an attacker walks a quiet path to domain admin. This episode is for identity, infrastructure and security teams who run on-premises AD. It walks through the most common misconfigurations, the attack chains they enable and the hardening, tiering and detection rituals that close them.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Unconstrained delegation and TGT extraction
  • 06:41 – Stale KRBTGT and golden ticket persistence
  • 13:07 – Overprivileged backup service and DCSync
  • 20:16 – Local admin reuse and pass-the-hash
  • 27:19 – SMB signing, NTLM relay and LSASS harvest
  • 41:47 – GPP passwords, forest trusts and AD CS ESC1
  • 65:39 – Monday actions: tiering, hardening and detection
  • 86:20 – Legacy, PAC validation, segmentation and response

Key takeaways

  • Active Directory drifts toward insecurity through convenience: unconstrained delegation, stale KRBTGT keys, overprivileged backup accounts and shared local admin passwords each open a quiet path to domain dominance.
  • Close those paths with constrained or resource-based delegation, twice-rotated KRBTGT, gMSAs, Windows LAPS, SMB signing, LSA protection and Credential Guard, and remove GPP passwords, open trusts and loose certificate templates.
  • Enforce tiering with privileged access workstations, tune detections on Kerberos, replication and LSASS events, and run contained, evidence-first response rituals instead of improvised clean-ups.

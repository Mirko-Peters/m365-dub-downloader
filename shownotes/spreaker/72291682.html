Connecting Copilot to millions of documents does not make answers appear; without the right retrieval architecture, noise, latency and permission leaks take over. This episode is for architects and enterprise leaders planning Copilot or RAG at extreme scale. Learn how tiering, structure-aware chunking, hybrid retrieval, routing and built-in governance make massive datasets searchable and trustworthy.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – The data blindness problem
  • 03:29 – Why standard RAG collapses at scale
  • 06:44 – Selective activation and recursive structure-aware chunking
  • 13:41 – Multimodal ingestion, entity extraction and knowledge graphs
  • 20:40 – Agentic routing, hybrid retrieval and permission-aware retrieval
  • 32:05 – Benchmarks, latency and metadata enrichment
  • 48:05 – Orchestration, cost, governance and real-world rollout
  • 67:00 – Failure modes, monitoring, future directions and key takeaways

Key takeaways

  • At millions of pages, plugging Copilot into data is not enough: a tiered selective-activation model plus recursive, structure-aware chunking with overlap beats expensive semantic chunking on cost and accuracy.
  • Precision comes from orchestration: an intent-based router, hybrid BM25 and vector retrieval fused with reciprocal rank fusion, cross-encoder reranking, rich metadata and knowledge graphs.
  • Governance and measurement must be built in from day one, including chunk-level permission trimming, sensitivity labels, retention, audit logs, latency budgets and gold-standard evaluation sets.

Worth quoting

“This is what I call data blindness. The data is not missing, but it is everywhere at once, which ends up being the exact same thing.” – Mirko Peters (01:29)

“The paradox surfaces right here, more data without orchestration does not lead to better answers. It leads to worse ones.” – Mirko Peters (06:03)

Production plans that look fine in ERP fall apart when a machine fails, a fixture is busy or material is still in inspection. This episode walks manufacturing planners, IT/OT architects and operations leaders through constraint-based scheduling in a hypothetical machining plant, showing how to turn factory facts into rules a scheduling engine can test and produce schedules people can actually run.

In this episode

  • 06:20 – Plan versus schedule

  • 68:55 – What the scheduling engine solves
  • 79:42 – When the five-axis machine fails and constraints collide
  • 102:30 – ERP, MES, the scheduling layer and Microsoft architecture

Key takeaways

  • An ERP date states intent; a finite, constraint-based schedule tests every operation against real resources, tooling, skills, material release and time windows.
  • Hard constraints decide feasibility first, while soft constraints and visible priority policies decide which feasible schedule the plant prefers.
  • ERP, MES and the scheduling layer each keep their own job, with Microsoft Fabric and Power BI providing governed history and visibility rather than acting as the scheduling engine.

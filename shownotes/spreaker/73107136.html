Traditional antivirus can no longer keep up with ransomware, phishing and attackers who move quietly between devices. This plain-English episode is for IT admins and business owners who want to understand Microsoft Defender for Endpoint, from EDR and attack disruption to licensing and the first steps to turn it on.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why antivirus is no longer enough
  • 00:37 – What endpoint security means
  • 02:30 – Defender for Endpoint: prevention, detection, response
  • 04:56 – Endpoint detection and response (EDR)
  • 06:34 – Vulnerability management and threat analytics
  • 07:52 – Automated investigation and attack disruption
  • 10:09 – How it fits into Microsoft Defender XDR
  • 11:49 – How to get started

Key takeaways

  • Signature-based antivirus only catches known threats, so modern endpoint security has to watch behavior across every device, not just scan files.
  • Microsoft Defender for Endpoint combines next-generation protection, EDR, vulnerability management and automated investigation, and Plan 2 is already included in Microsoft 365 E5.
  • Integrated with Defender XDR, Intune and Sentinel, it correlates alerts into single incidents, but small teams still need people or an MDR service to watch and respond.

Worth quoting

“Signature-based detection only catches what's already been seen. Modern attacks are adaptive, targeted, and often use brand new or custom malware.” – Mirko Peters (01:48)

“Prevention is the lock on your front door. And EDR is the motion sensor in your hallway that alerts you when someone's already inside.” – Mirko Peters (05:08)

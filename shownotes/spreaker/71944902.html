Most FinOps teams still rely on alerts, dashboards and month-end reviews while Azure spend grows in real time. This episode shows cloud platform, governance and FinOps teams how to turn Azure Policy from passive audit mode into a real-time enforcement gate that blocks wasteful deployments before they ever reach the bill.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why your Azure bill goes wrong before finance notices
  • 00:56 – Why reactive FinOps keeps failing
  • 02:57 – What Azure Policy actually does and where it breaks
  • 06:10 – The enforcement model: turning policy into a budget machine
  • 10:10 – Where policy-driven FinOps programs go wrong
  • 13:20 – The rollout path that preserves velocity
  • 16:15 – The metrics that prove it is working

Key takeaways

  • Cost alerts and dashboards provide visibility, but only enforcement at the Azure Resource Manager request path, such as Azure Policy deny effects, actually prevents waste before billing starts.
  • Encode financial intent as policy: deny high-cost SKUs and regions where they do not belong, use modify to inherit cost tags, and package rules into initiatives assigned through management groups.
  • Roll out in stages - time-boxed audit, platform and template repair, deny in non-production, then production with owned, expiring exceptions - and measure prevented cost and cost-weighted adherence rather than compliance percentages.

Worth quoting

“Reactive FinOps fails because it still assumes reporting is control. It isn't. What most teams do is simple enough. They observe spend, build reports, explain variances, raise questions and then try to clean things up after the fact.” – Mirko Peters (00:42)

“Use deny for the things that are never allowed under any circumstances. Use modify where metadata should be inherited or corrected during the request, especially for tags that need to flow down from a resource group.” – Mirko Peters (07:53)

Your Copilot gives fluent, cited answers that are quietly wrong, and everyone blames the model. This deep dive for Microsoft 365 architects and governance teams shows how retrieval, permissions, prompts and governance theatre build a hallucination machine, and which controls dismantle it.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Hallucination is an architecture problem
  • 00:47 – Why LLMs hallucinate by design
  • 06:18 – Retrieval and grounding as hallucination engines
  • 16:07 – The orchestration anti-pattern and prompt engineering
  • 26:49 – Governance theatre and retrieval collapse
  • 41:23 – Permission sprawl, data quality and agent drift
  • 58:45 – Fixing it: retrieval, grounding and orchestration controls
  • 74:11 – Continuous evaluation and drift detection

Key takeaways

  • Copilot hallucinations stem mostly from orchestration, retrieval and data-quality choices, not from the language model alone.
  • Permission sprawl, unlabeled content and stale documents turn Microsoft 365 into a source of confident but wrong answers.
  • Retrieval-first governance, grounding as a hard constraint, validation gates and golden-dataset regression testing keep Copilot reliable over time.

Worth quoting

“You can build perfect orchestration around terrible data and you'll still produce hallucinations but you can't build perfect data and survive without orchestration.” – Mirko Peters (70:45)

“Retrieval governance controls what the model can see but orchestration controls what the model can do. It's the difference between limiting what goes in and limiting what comes out both are mandatory.” – Mirko Peters (69:57)

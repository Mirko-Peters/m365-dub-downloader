Is Fabric Data Factory just Azure Data Factory with a new name? This episode explains the four building blocks - pipelines, Dataflow Gen2, copy jobs and mirroring - and how they work together through OneLake. Data engineers and analysts get a clear rule of thumb for when switching from ADF pays off.

In this episode

  • 10:18 – How the pieces work together
  • 11:49 – Practical guidance and cost

Key takeaways

  • Fabric Data Factory is the built-in data integration engine of Microsoft Fabric, with capacity-based pricing and OneLake as automatic storage instead of linked services and storage accounts.
  • Pipelines orchestrate, Dataflow Gen2 transforms with Power Query, copy jobs handle continuous incremental ingestion and mirroring replicates operational databases in near real time.
  • If your ADF bill is low and you are not on Fabric, staying with ADF can make sense; if you already have Fabric capacity, pipelines cost very little extra.

Worth quoting

“ADF charges per activity run, so every copy activity stored procedure and pipeline execution adds to your bill. Fabric Data Factory uses capacity-based pricing instead.” – Mirko Peters (01:32)

“If pipelines are the assembly line that moves boxes around, data flow, Gen 2 is the kitchen where you actually chop, season, and cook the raw ingredients.” – Mirko Peters (05:20)

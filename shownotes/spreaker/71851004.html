Managed Environments were supposed to bring order to the Power Platform, yet many organizations see critical apps stall and pro developers walk away. This episode is for Power Platform admins, architects and IT leaders who pay for governance but struggle to ship. Learn why governance needs a pro-dev spine with ALM, CI/CD and disciplined integration to deliver safely.

In this episode

  • 00:00 – Why Managed Environments promise control but stall delivery
  • 01:06 – The ghost town effect of locked governance
  • 04:18 – The hidden wall between low-code and pro-code
  • 07:38 – Why delivery fails without embedded CI/CD
  • 11:08 – How integration debt turns managed platforms into liabilities
  • 15:05 – Why AI and Copilot make the gap impossible to ignore
  • 18:20 – The model that works: governance with a pro-dev spine

Key takeaways

  • Managed Environments govern access, monitoring and policy, but they do not replace source control, build validation, release approvals or rollback paths.
  • Enterprise Power Platform apps need ALM through CI/CD, for example with Azure DevOps, plus a governed integration layer with clear endpoint ownership instead of scattered connector logic.
  • Zone workloads into maker-led, fusion and pro-dev tiers and measure release frequency, failed deployments and orphaned apps instead of inventory counts, especially before scaling AI and Copilot.

Worth quoting

“Low code changes how you build. It does not remove the need to manage change.” – Mirko Peters (06:26)

“Managed environments add order around the container, but CICD controls movement through the container. Those are different jobs. One helps govern the space, the other governs change.” – Mirko Peters (08:42)

## FAQ

**When does a manufacturer need production planning software?**

When changes, shared resources and dependencies arrive faster than planners can test the consequences manually. Company size or headcount is not the trigger. Warning signs include plans changing faster than replanning, capacity that exists only on paper, conflicting order dates, moving bottlenecks, routine expediting and planning rules that live only in people's heads.

**Can ERP handle detailed production scheduling on its own?**

Usually not. ERP owns demand, bills of material, inventory, purchasing and production orders, and MRP with rough-cut capacity planning gives useful dates and load views. But a weekly load picture cannot choose the exact sequence that respects setups, maintenance windows, staffing and resource limits. A planning layer tests that and feeds results back to ERP.

**Where does Microsoft Fabric fit in production planning?**

Fabric wraps around the planning engine rather than replacing it. It brings ERP, MES, quality, maintenance and IoT data into a governed foundation, supports data quality checks before data reaches scheduling, and enables plan versus actual analysis over time. It does not generate a feasible schedule; that logic stays in the planning engine and the factory's rules.

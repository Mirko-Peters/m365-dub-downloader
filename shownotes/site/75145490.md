## FAQ

**What is the difference between a production plan and a production schedule?**

A production plan states what the business wants to achieve, grouping demand at work center level over weeks or months. A finite schedule assigns each operation to a real resource in a real sequence, with start and finish times that fit calendars, fixtures, qualified people and released material, so the work can actually run.

**What are hard and soft constraints in production scheduling?**

Hard constraints are conditions production cannot validly ignore, such as safety rules, unreleased material, a reserved fixture or a missing operator qualification. Soft constraints express preferences or costs, like lateness, setup changes or overtime. The scheduler first finds feasible schedules that respect every hard limit, then uses soft constraints to choose between them.

**Can ERP or Microsoft Fabric replace a dedicated scheduling engine?**

No, the episode argues each system keeps its own job. ERP holds demand, work orders and commercial commitments, MES records execution, and the scheduling layer decides what should run next. Microsoft Fabric provides governed history such as setup durations and schedule adherence, and Power BI makes schedule health visible, but neither is a finite scheduling engine.

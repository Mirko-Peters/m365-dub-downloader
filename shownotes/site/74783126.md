## Key takeaways

- Copilot does not create a new mess but amplifies years of Microsoft 365 sprawl in permissions, ownership, lifecycle, guest access and connectors.
- Governance should be continuous, distributed and recoverable: notify department leads, phase remediation, keep actions restorable and use agents or tools to scale the work.
- Instead of a one-time AI readiness checklist, measure sprawl maturity, use migrations and consolidations to clean up, and make sure governance of agents scales with how fast they are created.

## FAQ

**Does Microsoft 365 Copilot create oversharing problems?**

No, Copilot mostly exposes and amplifies sprawl that already exists. Richard Harbridge compares tenants to layers of sediment built from restructures, mergers and temporary permission decisions that became permanent. ShareGate survey data he cites shows 93 percent of IT leaders feel confident in their governance, while 29 percent say Copilot surfaced content it should not have.

**How should we clean up old sharing links and inactive sites before Copilot?**

Start with visibility, then remediate in phases and keep every action recoverable. Richard suggests reporting old anonymous or guest links, deleting those well past your expiration baseline, and letting people reshare if needed. For inactive sites, notify owners and department leads first, archive rather than delete, and make sure automation can be restored.

**What is the difference between AI readiness and AI fitness?**

AI fitness is a continuous practice, while readiness sounds like a finished checklist. Richard splits the idea into ambition, what you want to do with AI, fitness, whether you can support it responsibly, and acceleration, how quickly you turn it into value. As ambition rises, governance fitness has to rise with it.

## FAQ

**When should a machine stop trigger production re-planning?**

Only a confirmed loss of capacity should trigger re-planning. Fault codes, short stops and planned downtime usually do not. The episode recommends judging a stop by expected capacity loss, recovery window and the orders exposed, letting the local team handle it first, and asking operators, maintenance and the MES to confirm unclear states.

**What is finite capacity scheduling and why does it matter after a breakdown?**

Finite capacity scheduling treats every machine, person, tool and time window as limited, so no slot is claimed twice. After an outage it marks the failed machine as unavailable and checks material arrival, setups, labor calendars and sequence. Infinite capacity plans can show orders on time while hiding an impossible queue on the shop floor.

**Can Microsoft Fabric and Power BI reschedule production automatically?**

No, they provide the facts and visibility, not the scheduling logic. Microsoft Fabric brings machine events, MES progress, ERP orders and maintenance status into a governed data layer, and Power BI shows outages and exposure. A scheduling or optimization service evaluates constraints and proposes options, while people approve the changes that reach the shop floor.

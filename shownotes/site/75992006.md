## FAQ

**What is the difference between traceability, genealogy and provenance in manufacturing?**

Traceability lets you follow recorded history, such as where material moved and which operations a product passed. Genealogy describes how inputs actually contribute to outputs through assembly or transformation. Provenance concerns origin and the evidence behind a claim. Separating them matters because a transfer to a station proves movement, not that a seal ended up inside a specific pump.

**Should I use batch or serial tracking for traceability?**

Choose based on failure risk, customer obligations and what the process can physically preserve, not on label count. Batch tracking suits interchangeable parts that share processing conditions, while serial tracking distinguishes individual units. A mixed model often works, for example serialized pumps linked to seal batches, but only if consumption is captured per pump rather than per order.

**How do you prevent duplicate consumption events after a connection failure?**

Give every event a unique identifier before its first transmission and keep it through retries, so the receiver can recognize a repeated delivery instead of recording a second physical action. This idempotent processing needs the duplicate check and the record update to commit together, plus durable local buffering, controlled replay and reconciliation after an outage.

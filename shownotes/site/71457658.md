## Key takeaways

- Frictionless automation removes the effort that used to force questions about ownership, exceptions and process design, so it often hides and scales the mess instead of fixing it.
- Provisioning sprawl, Power Automate approval chains with dozens of exceptions and Copilot on top of bad information architecture all show the same pattern: organizations automate their inconsistencies and build up process debt.
- Track the exception rate, give every automation a named business owner, add expiry and review rules, log exceptions by default and review new automation patterns instead of every individual build.

## FAQ

**What is the exception rate in automation and how do you measure it?**

The exception rate is the number of manual overrides and flow breaks divided by the total number of automated executions. Under 5% is usually healthy, between 5 and 15% means you should ask what changed in the business, and above 15% means the automation is scaling a mismatch rather than consistency.

**What is process debt?**

Process debt is the accumulated set of shortcuts in decision rights, review cycles and ownership that ends up inside automated workflows. It includes old exceptions nobody retired and temporary fixes that became permanent logic. Low-code platforms make it look manageable because the visual workflow is readable, but readable is not the same as simple.

**Why can Microsoft 365 Copilot make bad information architecture worse?**

Copilot retrieves whatever it has permission to access but cannot tell whether content is current, complete or owned. On top of duplicated files and abandoned spaces, it turns inconsistent documents into fluent answers. Because the effort drops, users check less, so weak content reaches decisions faster and with unearned confidence.

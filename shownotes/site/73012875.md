## Key takeaways

- Standing privileges leave admin rights active around the clock although they are used only briefly, turning every compromised admin account into a potential disaster.
- PIM makes users eligible instead of permanently active, so roles are activated just in time with a time limit, [MFA](https://www.m365.fm/entity/mfa/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=privileged-identity-management-pim-simply-explained), justification and optional approval, and every activation is audited.
- Start by making your global admin accounts eligible, keep activation windows short, require MFA on activation and keep separate break-glass accounts.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between eligible and active assignments in PIM?**

An eligible assignment means a user may request a role, while an active assignment means they already hold it. Eligible users must activate the role first, typically passing MFA, giving a justification and sometimes getting approval. A stolen eligible account therefore gives an attacker no admin power, unlike a permanently active one.

**How long should a PIM role activation last?**

Keep activations as short as practical: about one hour for most tasks and around four hours for bigger maintenance windows. Admins can set a maximum per role between 30 minutes and 24 hours, but a 24 hour window is essentially permanent access. Anything longer than a few hours needs serious justification.

**Do I still need break-glass accounts when using Privileged Identity Management?**

Yes, keep one or two emergency accounts with standing global admin access that bypass PIM. They protect you when an approver is unavailable, a policy breaks or someone gets locked out. Give them long, complex passwords, store the credentials securely, monitor them heavily and never use them in normal operations.

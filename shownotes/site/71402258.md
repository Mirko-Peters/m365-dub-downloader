Multi-tenant organizations often assume one vendor and one identity estate mean one AI control story, but Copilot exposes every governance gap between tenants. This episode is for IT leaders, architects and security teams rolling out Microsoft 365 Copilot across several tenants. You will learn why manual per-tenant governance fails and how a hub-and-spoke model with clear rollout gates lets you scale safely.

## Key takeaways

- Multiple [Microsoft 365](https://www.m365.fm/entity/microsoft-365/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=the-multi-tenant-copilot-trap-mastering-global-ai-governance) tenants are separate governance systems: Copilot works within one tenant boundary, and shared identity or cross-tenant settings do not make policies, labels or audit consistent.
- A federated hub-and-spoke model works best: the hub owns policy design, audit baselines, label standards and rollout gates, while spoke teams remediate inside each tenant.
- Before scaling Copilot, measure oversharing reduction, observability coverage, time to policy across tenants, label coverage and access drift, and pause any tenant that cannot prove logging, oversharing reviews and label baselines.

## FAQ

**Can Microsoft 365 Copilot work across multiple tenants at once?**

No. Microsoft 365 Copilot operates within the security and compliance boundary of one tenant and respects the user's permissions in that tenant context. A user can switch context as a guest, but Copilot is still scoped to one tenant at a time and does not merge several tenants into one answer.

**What is a hub-and-spoke model for multi-tenant Copilot governance?**

It splits design from execution. A central hub owns policy design, audit baselines, label standards and rollout criteria, while spoke teams apply that baseline inside each tenant by running remediation, validating logs and reviewing broad access. Deviations go through a formal exception path, so the hub provides consistency and the spokes provide speed.

**What must be in place before rolling out Copilot in a tenant?**

Validated audit logging, a completed oversharing scan and a minimum baseline for label coverage. The episode treats this as a hard rule: if a tenant cannot prove all three, its rollout pauses. Planned or partially discussed controls do not count, because Copilot immediately uses whatever permissions and content exposure the tenant already allows.

## Key takeaways

- Hire-to-retire is not a linear process but a stream of facts, obligations and identity changes processed by many systems that make independent decisions.
- Policy hidden in templates, stages, connector conditions, flows and [Conditional Access](https://www.m365.fm/entity/conditional-access/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=debunking-hire-to-retire-myth-hr-systems) exceptions creates configuration entropy that AI will mirror instead of fix.
- A four-layer architecture with a versioned policy layer, immutable events, subscriber-only execution and an explanation-first AI layer lets HR own intent while platforms execute and Entra enforces.

## FAQ

**Why do AI pilots in HR systems stall at recommendation only?**

Because there is no explicit policy for the agent to cite. HR rules sit in workflow branches, connector filters, email templates and pick list labels, so the model learns those artifacts and the exceptions in them. It can draft and remind, but it cannot prove a decision complies, so autonomy never gets approved.

**Why does a terminated employee still have access after HR offboarding?**

Because access is decided in Microsoft Entra ID and the integration glue, not by the HR record alone. The episode describes provisioning flows that filtered or retried the termination event, Conditional Access exception groups that never expired, and direct app role assignments that no HR change ever touched.

**What are the four layers of the proposed HR reference architecture?**

A versioned, testable policy layer, an immutable event layer, an execution layer and an explanation-first AI layer. Flows and connectors only subscribe to rules and facts, every decision cites a policy version, and agents query the policy layer and event stream instead of handbooks, so they can explain or decline with evidence.

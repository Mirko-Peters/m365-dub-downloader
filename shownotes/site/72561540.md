## FAQ

**What is a computer-using agent and when should you use one?**

A computer-using agent is an AI model that reads a screen and clicks, types and navigates like a human. The episode recommends it as a bridge for legacy systems without APIs, called as a tool from Foundry. It succeeds on simple tasks 67 to 85 percent of the time but only 9 to 19 percent on complex multi-step workflows.

**Why do AI agents put seat-based SaaS pricing under pressure?**

Seat-based pricing assumes revenue grows with headcount, but agents decouple work from the number of people. In the episode's example, one agent handling invoice reconciliation does the work of five people, so the customer needs four fewer licenses. Vendors are therefore exploring consumption-based, outcome-based and hybrid pricing, which require metering and new billing systems.

**What is workflow capital and why does it matter for AI agents?**

Workflow capital is the accumulated operational logic that makes a company work: decision criteria, approval chains, exception handling and unwritten rules. The episode argues this, not the software itself, is the real competitive moat. Organizations should encode it in private or controlled agents and be careful about letting vendors train on their workflow data.

## Key takeaways

- Buying Copilot licenses is not enough; continuous, role-based training is what keeps people using AI after the first few weeks.
- Organizations should measure AI value against real business outcomes, prepare and label their data, and move step by step from informational agents to agents that take actions with guardrails.
- Agent sprawl, shadow AI and rising token costs call for lifecycle policies, good sanctioned tools and FinOps thinking about which model and tool to use for each task.

## FAQ

**Is buying Microsoft 365 Copilot licenses enough to get employees using it?**

No. Christoffer Besler Hansen says license-only rollouts do not work, because Copilot changes every week and users have other work to do. Atea runs training at least every other week, including role-based sessions for managers, consultants and sales. Before training started in early 2025, engagement typically faded after about four weeks.

**How should companies measure the business value of Copilot?**

Measure business outcomes, not just usage. Christoffer suggests defining what the company delivers and comparing speed and quality with and without AI. User surveys only capture a feeling. A stronger signal is a consultant at Atea who now handles six cases instead of two, with customers reporting better quality than before.

**How can you stop AI agents from taking risky actions such as deleting data?**

Set clear guardrails before agents act. Christoffer recommends a harness that defines which tasks are pre-approved and which require the agent to ask first, especially anything destructive. Agents should get least privilege rather than broad admin rights, and company-wide agents should pass an intake review of instructions, access and connectors.

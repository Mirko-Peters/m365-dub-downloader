## Key takeaways

- ExpressRoute is a private connection from your network into Microsoft's backbone that bypasses the public internet for predictable latency, high bandwidth and an SLA.
- Each circuit has built-in redundant links, but mission-critical workloads should use high or maximum resiliency across peering locations, optionally with a VPN as backup.
- Provisioning needs your connectivity provider and can take weeks, so start early and choose ExpressRoute only when performance, predictability or compliance justify the cost over a VPN.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between Azure ExpressRoute and a site-to-site VPN?**

ExpressRoute is a private connection that bypasses the public internet, while a VPN sends encrypted traffic over it. According to the episode, the top VPN gateway SKU reaches around 1.25 gigabits per second, whereas ExpressRoute goes up to 100 gigabits, offers predictable latency and comes with a 99.9% uptime SLA, but costs more.

**How long does it take to provision an ExpressRoute circuit?**

Creating the circuit in the Azure portal takes minutes, but the provider side can take days or weeks. You hand the service key to your connectivity provider, who configures the physical connection. Only when the provider status changes to provisioned can you set up peering, so order the circuit well before your migration window.

**Is Azure ExpressRoute traffic encrypted by default?**

No, ExpressRoute gives you a private path but does not automatically encrypt your traffic. A VPN has IPsec encryption built in. If compliance requires encryption at the network layer over the private link, the episode notes you need extra measures such as MACsec on ExpressRoute Direct, so plan for it early.

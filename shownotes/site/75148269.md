## Key takeaways

- ERP is the governed system of record for demand, supply, inventory and transactions, while APS tests whether the plan can actually run under finite capacity and real constraints.
- A feasible schedule depends on detailed constraint data such as calendars, qualified labor, tools, setup rules and material readiness, plus explicit business priority rules.
- APS supports rather than replaces the planner, and clear ownership across ERP, APS and MES matters more than the number of integrations.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between APS and ERP?**

ERP governs the business record: demand, supply, inventory, purchasing and financial transactions. APS, advanced planning and scheduling, takes that demand and tests whether it can actually run against finite capacity, shift calendars, qualified labor, tools, setup rules and material readiness. The two answer different questions and usually work together rather than compete for one job.

**When is ERP scheduling enough without separate APS software?**

ERP scheduling is often enough when routes are stable, the product range is small, demand is steady and capacity has some headroom. Rough cut capacity planning and simple finite scheduling, combined with strong planner routines, can give credible answers. Test it with your own recent cases, such as a rush order or a resource outage.

**Does APS software replace the production planner?**

No. APS can calculate far more schedule options than a planner could rebuild by hand, but people still define priority rules, approve overtime and judge commercial, safety and quality trade-offs. The planner spends less time shuffling orders and more time managing constraints and improving rules, which only works when APS can explain its results.

## Key takeaways

- Start a digital twin with a repeating decision and the person who makes it, then model only the entities, state and relationships that change that decision.
- Give every entity one stable twin ID, keep raw telemetry in time series storage and put only trusted current state with source, time and quality on the twin.
- Azure Digital Twins is the context layer that connects ERP, MES, APS and PLC facts through DTDL models and relationships, while each source system keeps ownership of its own facts.

## FAQ

**What should you model first in an Azure Digital Twins project?**

Start with a repeating decision and the person who makes it, not with an asset or a 3D scene. Then model only the entities, state and relationships that change that decision. The episode argues that an order twin, which connects a machine stop to customer demand and due dates, can beat a detailed machine twin.

**What is the difference between a digital twin and an IoT Hub device twin?**

A device twin stays focused on one device and its cloud connection, while a digital twin connects business context. The device twin does not know which order a machine runs or whether a stop blocks a downstream line. Azure Digital Twins builds a graph where equipment, orders, batches, locations and operations link together.

**Should raw PLC telemetry be stored on the digital twin?**

No, raw telemetry belongs in a time series store such as Azure Data Explorer or Microsoft Fabric. The twin holds trusted current state with source, time and quality, the fact someone needs to act on now. Azure Digital Twins does not interpret PLC signals itself, so that meaning must be defined upstream.

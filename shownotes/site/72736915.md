## FAQ

**What are Microsoft Graph activity logs used for in security?**

Graph activity logs record every request applications make to Microsoft Graph, including the app, endpoint, time, IP address and result. The episode uses them to check whether apps actually use the permissions they hold, to spot abnormal call spikes that suggest a compromised app, and to trace exactly what was accessed during an investigation.

**How can you safely revoke unused OAuth permissions in Microsoft 365?**

Revoke them methodically: inventory apps and grants through Graph, check activity logs for the scopes actually used, and assess business impact with app owners first. The episode recommends partial revocation, such as reducing read-write to read-only, then monitoring before full removal, and working in small batches rather than across every application at once.

**Is Microsoft Graph security data available in real time?**

No, the episode calls real-time a myth. Sign-in and audit logs typically arrive with a two-to-five-minute delay, alert latency varies by source, and Conditional Access changes can take up to 24 hours to propagate. The answer is defense in depth: layered controls, continuous access evaluation and pipelines designed to tolerate latency and failure.

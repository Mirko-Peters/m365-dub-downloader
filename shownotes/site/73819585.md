## FAQ

**What are the basic pillars of Power Platform governance?**

Michael Roth names four basics: tenant settings, environments, DLP policies, and roles and responsibilities. He adds a hidden fifth point: default settings are risky, because Power Platform starts open to everyone. Even organizations that only bought Dynamics licenses get a Power Platform underneath with no DLP and no environment structure until someone sets it up.

**What do DLP policies actually do in Power Platform?**

In Power Platform, DLP policies decide which connectors may be combined in one solution and which connectors are blocked entirely. That differs from DLP in Microsoft 365 or Teams. Policies apply per environment, so admins can be granular, and with premium licenses and Managed Environments, advanced connector policies give even finer control over what is allowed.

**How can admins build a Power Platform inventory without the CoE Starter Kit?**

Use the new native inventory in the Power Platform admin center, which queries the API directly and shows changes within roughly 10 to 15 minutes. It still lacks custom connectors and owner names. Larger organizations can build their own inventory on the Power Platform APIs, because the CoE Starter Kit will no longer receive updates.

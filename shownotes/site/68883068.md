## Key takeaways

- Triggers are the riskiest part of a flow, so gate them with trigger conditions, pre-filters and server-side queries and keep concurrency off until you own a partition key.
- Reliable flows follow one scaffold everywhere: try, catch and finally scopes, append-only logging with correlation IDs, named terminations, bounded retries with circuit breakers and a poison queue.
- Architect automation as a master orchestrator with light task routers and small idempotent child flows, owned by service principals and governed by [DLP](https://www.m365.fm/entity/dlp/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=power-automate-orchestrator-what-works), environments and regular owner sweeps.

## FAQ

**How do I stop a Power Automate flow from triggering itself in a loop?**

Add a trigger condition so the flow only acts on a real state change, such as status moving from not ready to ready. In the episode, a SharePoint flow that updated the item it listened to looped until sunrise. The fix added a last-processed hash column, a service principal owner, capped concurrency and a poison path.

**What is the best way to handle errors in Power Automate?**

Use one standard scaffold in every flow: try, catch and finally scopes. Try does the work, catch records status, stage, error code and the failing action, and finally cleans up and decides the run's fate. Log append-only with correlation IDs, terminate with named reason codes and send permanent failures to a poison queue.

**How should retries and circuit breakers work in Power Automate flows?**

Retries should follow bounded math, not hope: exponential back-off with a ceiling, about three tries for network blips and one for a hard 403. For a 503 the episode suggests waits like 10, 30 and 60 seconds, then the circuit breaker opens, the record is marked paused and a timed watcher tries again later.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between resilience and high availability in Azure?**

Resilience means a system bends under stress and recovers without breaking, like a palm tree in a storm, while high availability means a component breaks and a second instance elsewhere takes over. Mike Martin adds that resilience also covers load, scale and application errors, whereas availability mainly means the service responds and can fail over.

**Can an Azure solution reach a 100% SLA?**

No. Mike Martin says no solution reaches 100% because the composite SLA is the product of the SLAs of all dependent services, each below 100%, so adding services lowers the total. The goal is to get as close as possible with tested fallback plans, and to plan the return to normal operations after a failover.

**Does Microsoft make my Azure application resilient automatically?**

Not fully. Microsoft provides building blocks, such as storage accounts that keep three copies within a datacenter, but deploying VMs across zones or regions, or configuring failover for Azure Cosmos DB, is your design decision. Mike Martin notes many of these options are not enabled by default, so application resilience and monitoring remain your responsibility.

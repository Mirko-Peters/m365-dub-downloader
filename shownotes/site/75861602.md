## Key takeaways

- A PPR knowledge graph links product, process and resource facts from ERP, MES, historian and maintenance systems so an operating situation can be reconstructed instead of guessed.
- Planned and actual facts must stay separate and the model must preserve effective time, because the same machine, route or product name can mean different things after a revision or tool change.
- Standards like ISA-95, OPC UA and the Asset Administration Shell and platforms like Microsoft Fabric provide structure and plumbing, but the PPR semantics still need deliberate mappings from people who know the factory.

## FAQ

**What is a PPR knowledge graph in manufacturing?**

A PPR knowledge graph models how product, process and resource facts connect at a given moment. Product covers what is made, process the transformation, and resource the people, equipment, tooling and material involved. It links records from ERP, MES, historian and maintenance systems without replacing them, so those systems keep their authority.

**Can a knowledge graph prove the root cause of a quality defect?**

No, a graph alone cannot prove physical causation. It lets a user trace evidence from an undesired condition to the unit or lot, its actual operations, material, tool, resource state and earlier changes. An AI service can describe that dependency, but people still need process knowledge, validation and controls before acting.

**How do ISA-95, OPC UA and the Asset Administration Shell relate to a PPR graph?**

They provide structure, not a finished graph. ISA-95 offers models and terminology for enterprise and operations integration, OPC UA describes device-facing data, and Asset Administration Shells describe individual assets. The PPR graph links these descriptions into product and process context, but each organization still maps its own systems, identities and business rules.

## Key takeaways

- AI transformation needs a strategy from the top, an AI team or council and a transformation team, otherwise everyone builds their own isolated solutions without business value.
- Trust grows when leaders share where the organization is heading, involve employees in solving problems and give a clear framework for which AI tools and data may be used.
- Adoption is a continuous effort: agents need owners and up-to-date data, and champions help colleagues keep up with constant change.

## FAQ

**Why do many Copilot and AI rollouts fail to deliver business value?**

Because everyone builds their own way of working without a shared strategy. Femke Cornelissen explains that isolated personal productivity solutions create no synergy across the organization. Team workflows should become shared agents, for example in Copilot Studio, built on a common data platform so individual work connects and the company improves as a whole.

**How should leaders start an AI transformation?**

Start with a clear direction from the CEO, then form an AI council with the CTO, CHRO and transformation lead to choose priority initiatives. A transformation team, which can be virtual, handles adoption, communication and building, and one person per team owns transformation. Training follows, together with a policy that explains security and what is allowed.

**Who should own AI agents in an organization?**

Every agent needs a named owner who improves its responses and keeps its data up to date. Femke Cornelissen notes her company has around 1,200 agents for 500 employees, many without an owner. She recommends clear policies, including deleting experimental agents, and making responsibility visible, for example in an organigram that shows both agents and people.

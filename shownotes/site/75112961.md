## FAQ

**What is the difference between infinite and finite capacity planning?**

Infinite capacity planning, typical of ERP and MRP, places demand against dates without checking whether each machine, person and shift can do the work. Finite capacity scheduling only places work where usable time and resources exist. For example, 40 hours of machining planned into a week with 20 working hours looks fine in ERP but cannot run.

**When should you use forward scheduling versus backward scheduling?**

Use backward scheduling to test a promise and forward scheduling to decide what to release and run next. Backward scheduling starts from the due date and works back through each operation. Forward scheduling starts when material and resources are ready and shows the real finish date. The gap between both results is the decision the plant must make.

**Why should changeovers be scheduled as real work?**

Because setups consume the same resource hours as production. A finite schedule places each changeover as an operation on the resource, including the people, tools and first-piece checks it needs. Sequence-dependent changeovers vary with what ran before, so a family matrix of transition times works better than one average, which hides the long changeovers that break dates.

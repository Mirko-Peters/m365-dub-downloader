## FAQ

**Why do Managed Environments in Power Platform slow down delivery?**

Managed Environments slow delivery when they are treated as the whole operating model instead of a policy layer. They handle access, monitoring and policy for makers, but not source control, build validation, release approvals or rollback. Complex apps then stall in reviews and manual deployments, and pro developers move their work elsewhere.

**Are native Power Platform pipelines enough, or do I need Azure DevOps?**

Native pipelines are enough for smaller teams and simpler scenarios, but Azure DevOps matters once several developers, environments and approvals collide. It coordinates artifacts, service connections, secrets in Key Vault, reusable YAML and environment-specific release steps, so unmanaged solutions move into source control and managed artifacts are promoted through gated stages.

**How should Power Platform workloads be split between makers and pro developers?**

Split workloads into three zones by risk and reach. Low-risk departmental tools stay maker-led. Shared apps that cross teams or touch sensitive data use a fusion model, where business context stays with users and engineering shapes delivery. Operationally critical or externally integrated workloads need clear pro-dev ownership, even if parts run inside Power Platform.

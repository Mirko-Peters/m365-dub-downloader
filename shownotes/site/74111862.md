## Key takeaways

- Microsoft 365 governance is the balance between creativity, freedom and structure, and it works best in the background so people can collaborate without bureaucracy getting in the way.
- Governance is a team sport that needs a leadership sponsor, IT and security, and line-of-business owners, with different rules for areas such as HR and legal than for sales or marketing.
- Start early with basic policies such as naming conventions, ownership and lifecycle rules for Teams and guests, then monitor for drift and automate so sprawl stays under control as the organization grows.

## FAQ

**When should an organization start with Microsoft 365 governance?**

As early as possible, ideally with basic policies in place before rolling out Teams or OneDrive. Heiko Brenn calls waiting too long one of the biggest mistakes, because sprawl in teams and guests builds up quickly once a rollout starts. Smaller companies can begin with simplified rules and expand them as the organization grows.

**Who is responsible for Microsoft 365 governance?**

Governance is a team sport rather than an IT-only task. It needs a sponsor at leadership level who owns internal and external requirements, IT and security people who implement policies and handle reporting, and line-of-business owners who know how their areas work. HR and legal usually need stricter rules than sales or marketing.

**How can you detect governance drift in Microsoft 365?**

By monitoring against defined policies and alerting on unusual patterns. Heiko Brenn mentions bypassed naming conventions, a jump from around 15 new teams a week to 500, one user creating many guests, or sudden changes in SharePoint storage. Self-healing can automatically return an object to the state the governance policy defines.

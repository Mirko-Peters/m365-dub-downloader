## FAQ

**Why is full autonomy for DevOps agents considered a myth?**

Because current agentic systems still need human correction on 40 to 73 percent of decisions, according to the episode. The goal is appropriate, tiered autonomy: agents merge low-risk changes such as test-only edits alone, flag business logic changes for human approval, and only propose production deployments that a human signs off.

**What is the supervisor-worker pattern for AI agents in DevOps?**

It is an architecture where one supervisor agent owns the goal and outcome while specialized workers handle testing, security, deployment strategy or performance. The supervisor splits work into tasks, combines results, enforces policies and makes the final call, which keeps accountability clear and lets teams audit or fix individual workers separately.

**When does agentic DevOps justify its higher cost?**

When the workflow is both high volume and high variance, such as incident response. The episode puts production-grade agentic systems at $10,000 to $50,000 to start versus $2,000 to $8,000 for traditional automation, with payback typically between months four and eight and 300 to 500 percent ROI over three years.

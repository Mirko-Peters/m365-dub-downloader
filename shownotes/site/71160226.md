## FAQ

**Why does manual Microsoft 365 governance fail at scale?**

Manual governance fails because it is queue-based: the action happens first and the review comes later, so risk lives in the gap between them. As Teams, SharePoint sites, flows and Copilot usage grow, decisions multiply faster than central teams can inspect them, and people route around slow approvals just to get their work done.

**What should you automate first in Microsoft 365 governance?**

Start with one high-frequency decision that has clear rules and visible friction, such as external sharing or team creation. In the episode's anchor case, the first moves were auto-labeling sensitive content, tighter DLP for high-risk sharing and automated workspace provisioning that required ownership, naming and purpose at creation, so each space was born governed.

**How can leaders measure whether governance automation works?**

Track the exposure window, meaning the time from a risky event to containment, and the manual governance load spent on approvals, reviews and escalations. Mirko Peters adds workspace quality, such as complete ownership and consistent lifecycle controls, and automation effectiveness, meaning how often the environment prevents, nudges or contains issues without human intervention.

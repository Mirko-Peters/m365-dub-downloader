## FAQ

**What is Active Directory tiering?**

Active Directory tiering boxes systems into administrative zones to prevent privilege escalation and lateral movement. Tier zero holds everything that manages the domain, such as domain controllers, Microsoft Entra Connect and PKI servers, while tier one holds workload servers. Higher-privileged accounts are blocked from logging on to lower tiers, which limits credential exposure.

**What should you do if you suspect your network has been compromised?**

Isolate systems by unplugging network cables and cutting outbound internet access, but do not turn anything off. Viktor explains that logs on the systems, including firewalls that keep logs only in memory, are essential for the forensic investigation. Then call an experienced incident response company instead of trying to fix things yourself.

**Should a PAM solution manage tier zero in Active Directory?**

Viktor recommends against it. A PAM solution depends on a service account with domain admin level permissions, and if that service goes down you may lose the ability to administer your environment. He suggests targeting PAM at tier one, where most administrative users are, and keeping PAM and IAM tools away from tier zero.

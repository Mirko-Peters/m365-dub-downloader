## FAQ

**Why is my Microsoft Fabric bill rising when my data volume is flat?**

Because Fabric bills compute from a shared capacity pool, not stored bytes. Costs follow workload behavior: query design, refresh overlap and concurrency. Smoothing spreads long background operations over up to 24 hours, and carry forward overage keeps affecting capacity after a burst, so a short spike can cause delays and cost long afterward.

**How do I find out what is consuming Fabric capacity units?**

Open the Capacity Metrics app and rank consumption by item instead of staring at the invoice total. Then compare the interactive versus background split, the time-of-day pattern and the throttling timeline. Spikes repeating every 30 to 60 minutes usually point to refresh overlap or pipeline collisions rather than a capacity that is simply too small.

**Should I buy more Fabric capacity or rely on overage to fix throttling?**

Usually not as the first move. Extra capacity or routine overage hides waste instead of removing it, so expensive queries and colliding refreshes keep growing. Overage works as a safety net for real bursts such as quarter-end reporting. When pressure is clustered and repetitive, fix SQL routing and refresh orchestration before buying more capacity.

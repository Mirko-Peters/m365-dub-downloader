Every Azure VM needs disk storage, and the wrong tier means either overpaying or slow performance. This episode explains Standard SSD, Premium SSD, Premium SSD v2 and Ultra Disk for Azure beginners and admins, with a simple decision guide and cost tips.

## Key takeaways

- IOPS, throughput and latency determine disk performance, and the goal is to match them to the workload without over- or under-provisioning.
- Premium SSD v2 decouples capacity, IOPS and throughput and covers most production databases at a lower price than Ultra Disk.
- Ultra Disk is reserved for extreme workloads such as SAP HANA or trading platforms, while Standard SSD is enough for dev/test and low-traffic servers.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between Premium SSD and Premium SSD v2 on Azure?**

Premium SSD ties performance to disk size, while Premium SSD v2 lets you adjust capacity, IOPS and throughput independently. With Premium SSD you may buy a bigger disk just for more IOPS. Premium SSD v2 includes 3,000 IOPS and 125 MB/s baseline and scales without downtime, though it currently supports only locally redundant storage.

**When do I actually need Azure Ultra Disk?**

You need Ultra Disk only for extreme workloads such as SAP HANA, high-frequency trading or health record systems. It offers up to 400,000 IOPS per disk and microsecond latency. For most workloads it is overkill, so the episode advises starting with Premium SSD v2 and moving up only if monitoring shows the need.

**Is Standard SSD good enough for an Azure VM?**

Standard SSD is good enough for dev and test environments, low-traffic web servers and applications where small delays do not matter. It has the lowest cost per gigabyte among SSD tiers. For production databases like SQL Server, Oracle or MySQL, or latency-sensitive applications, the episode recommends stepping up to a Premium tier instead.

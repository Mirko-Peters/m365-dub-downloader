## Key takeaways

- Microsoft Purview Records Management keeps official records for the required time and disposes of them in a controlled, reviewable way.
- Retention labels attach item-level rules (trigger, period, end action), record declaration locks official evidence, and retention policies cover broad locations.
- Disposition review and Purview Audit provide the decision gate and the trail needed to prove why a record was kept or deleted.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a retention label and a retention policy in Microsoft Purview?**

A retention policy applies one broad rule to a whole location, while a retention label attaches a specific rule to an individual item. Policies suit general content such as mailboxes, SharePoint sites or Teams messages. Labels suit official records that need their own path, especially when the clock starts from a business event like a contract ending.

**What happens during disposition review in Purview Records Management?**

When a record reaches the end of its retention period, Purview can send it to designated reviewers instead of deleting it. Reviewers check for disputes, legal requests or business reasons to keep it, then approve disposal or pause it. Multistage reviews are possible, and assigning a maintained group as reviewers avoids stalled queues.

**Does a record label encrypt or restrict access to a document?**

No, a record label controls how long official evidence is kept and which record controls apply, not who can open it. Encryption and access limits come from sensitivity labels, and data loss prevention watches for risky sharing. All three can apply to the same signed contract, but each one solves a different problem.

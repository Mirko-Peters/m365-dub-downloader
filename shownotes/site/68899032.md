Quick Adaptive Card Extensions in Teams and Viva Connections look harmless in demos but turn dashboards into a graveyard of orphaned, unlabeled cards. This episode is for Microsoft 365 architects, SharePoint and SPFx developers and governance owners who need a reference architecture, a decision tree and a checklist to approve or block card requests without becoming the villain.

## Key takeaways

- Adaptive Card Extensions are a distribution layer, not an application, so data, ownership, placement and lifecycle must be governed underneath the card.
- A governed reference architecture uses Entra ID groups, separate environments, canonical data contracts in SharePoint or Dataverse, [Purview](https://www.m365.fm/entity/purview/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=stop-building-apps-in-teams-spfx-aces) labels, CI/CD deployment and telemetry so cards stay disposable.
- Approve a card only when it reads from a governed store under a standard contract, has two owners, lifecycle dates, a scoped placement and a rollback path; otherwise route it to a real project.

## FAQ

**Why do SPFx Adaptive Card Extensions become a governance problem?**

They ship fast but have no built-in life cycle, so cards outlive their owners and data. The episode names five recurring failures: app sprawl, orphaned owners, data silos from per-card lists and schemas, compliance gaps from unlabeled content on mobile, and broken life cycle where content never expires and dashboards fill with stale cards.

**When should an Adaptive Card Extension request be approved?**

Approve it only when the card reads from a governed store under a standard data contract and only reads and renders. It also needs two owners, content and placement end dates, a scoped placement record, label and DLP checks, CI/CD deployment and a rollback path. Anything that writes data belongs in Power Apps or a real project.

**What does a governed reference architecture for Adaptive Card Extensions include?**

It treats the card as a thin UI skin over seven layers. These are Entra ID groups and separate dev, test and prod environments, canonical content contracts, controlled ingestion pipelines, Microsoft Purview guardrails, CI/CD with a placement registry, telemetry in Application Insights or Log Analytics, and automated life cycle for content, placements and solutions.

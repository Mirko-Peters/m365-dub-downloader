## FAQ

**How do you calculate the technical debt ratio for Power Automate?**

The technical debt ratio is the cost to fix your automation debt divided by your total automation investment, multiplied by 100. Under 5 percent means flows are clean and sustainable; above 5 percent, automation consumes value instead of delivering it. Mirko Peters pairs it with the share of orphaned flows and remediation time versus build time.

**What does ESOAR stand for in process automation?**

ESOAR stands for eliminate, standardize, optimize, automate and robotize, a gate applied before anything gets built. Teams first remove redundant processes, then simplify and standardize the business rules, and only then automate. The episode pairs it with modular child flows, so shared tasks like notifications or error logging are maintained in one place instead of many.

**Can Copilot clean up messy Power Automate flows later?**

No. According to the episode, Copilot speeds up flow creation but does nothing for accountability or architecture. AI-generated flows can create prompt logic debt that their makers don't understand. Mirko Peters recommends prompt versioning, human reviewers who understand the generated logic, and publishing restrictions that require a verified owner and a deprecation plan before anything goes live.

## Key takeaways

- Governance, landing zones and permissions need to be planned from day one, because redesigning a fast-growing production environment later is far harder and rarely gets prioritized.
- Security incidents and rising costs are the triggers that most often convince management to invest in governance, so frame proposals around those two risks.
- Keep it simple: a small set of well-chosen policies, a proper naming convention and automation-first deployments cover most of what matters, while experience prevents over-engineering.

## FAQ

**When should a company start planning Azure governance?**

On day zero, before workloads are built. Vladimir Stefanovic compares late governance to choosing tiles before planning the foundation. Many things can be fixed later, but some Azure resources cannot easily be moved, and a fast-growing production environment rarely leaves time or people to redesign subscriptions, networking and permissions once the business is pushing for new features.

**How do you convince management to invest in cloud governance?**

Frame it around security incidents and costs, the two triggers that most often move management. Governance work is hard to justify because it does not generate money directly, while new apps do. Showing concrete monthly savings or the impact of a breach, including lost client trust, makes the value far more visible to non-technical decision makers.

**Why does a naming convention matter in Azure?**

A proper naming convention makes environments standardized, and standardized environments are much easier to automate. Vladimir describes a setup where a short site acronym in a parameter file is enough to deploy a complete landing zone identical to the previous one. He calls naming and tagging underrated parts of governance that support everything else.

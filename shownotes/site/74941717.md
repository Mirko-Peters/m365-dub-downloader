## FAQ

**What is the critical path in a production order?**

It is the chain of remaining dependencies that sets the earliest possible completion date for an order under current factory conditions. Unlike a fixed project label, it moves when a machine stops, material is held or a quality check fails. Operations with zero float sit on it, and low-float work can join it quickly.

**Why does a production routing not show the critical path?**

A routing describes how a part should move through production, not when it can actually move. It lists operations, work centers and standard times, but ignores furnace load windows, queues, transfer batch rules, operator availability and alternate machines that are not truly ready. A short inspection before a shipping cutoff can control delivery more than long machining.

**What is the difference between a digital twin and a knowledge graph in manufacturing?**

A digital twin describes the state of one asset, such as a machining center's status, capabilities and active program. A knowledge graph records how orders, operations, resources, materials, quality rules and shipments connect, so it can trace what depends on that machine. Neither replaces the MES, the ERP or a scheduling engine; both provide structure.

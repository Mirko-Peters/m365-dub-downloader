Can certifications really build a career in Power Platform, or does only real-world experience count? This episode is for makers, consultants and IT professionals planning their next step in the Microsoft ecosystem. Microsoft MVP and MCT Nathalie Leenders shares how she moved from IT service management to Power Platform consulting and how to learn for exams the right way.

## Key takeaways

- Certifications are most valuable when used as a reason to learn deeply, combining Microsoft Learn, other sources and hands-on testing, rather than memorizing practice exam answers.
- Becoming a Microsoft Certified Trainer means learning how to teach rather than just present information, while the MVP award had the bigger career impact through community connections.
- Low-code is still code: start with canvas apps, understand Dataverse and [Power Automate](https://www.m365.fm/entity/power-automate/?utm_source=podcast&utm_medium=shownotes&utm_campaign=from-exams-to-expertise-building-a-career-in-power-platform), look at the bigger Microsoft picture and above all stay curious.

## FAQ

**Is PL-400 a good first Power Platform exam?**

Usually not; Nathalie Leenders recommends starting with the fundamentals and following your interests. She began with PL-400 herself after a colleague's remark felt like a challenge, studied for four months and passed, but calls it the broadest and most difficult Power Platform exam, covering JavaScript, plugins and PCF components beyond everyday app building.

**How do you become a Microsoft Certified Trainer?**

Nathalie became an MCT by completing a four-day train-the-trainer course that ended with an assessed lesson, which made her eligible to enroll. She notes the process recently changed. MCTs re-enroll every year based on trainee survey feedback, and the enrollment costs money, which her employer pays because she delivers trainings for it.

**Should you take a Microsoft exam at home or in a test center?**

Nathalie prefers a test center, even though it means driving about an hour each way. It avoids camera checks and clearing her workspace for an online proctored exam. The host shares that his own home exam lost about 20 minutes to camera and lighting problems, which left him stressed during the test.
